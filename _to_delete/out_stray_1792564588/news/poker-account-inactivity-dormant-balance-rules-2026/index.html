Poker Account Inactivity: Dormant Balance Rules

Idle money at an offshore poker room can trigger inactivity fees or expired bonuses. Learn how dormant account rules work and how to protect your funds.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 12, 2026·7 min read

Most players think carefully about which room to join and which welcome offer to claim, but very few think about what happens when they stop playing. Life gets busy, a season of poker ends, or you simply move your action to another site. Meanwhile, a balance sits untouched in the cashier. At many online poker rooms, an idle account is not treated the same as an active one, and the terms and conditions often contain clauses about inactivity that can quietly affect your money, your bonuses and your loyalty status.

This guide explains the general ideas behind dormant account policies, what to look for in a room's terms, and the practical habits that keep your bankroll safe. Rules differ between operators and change over time, so treat this as a framework for reading the fine print rather than a list of exact numbers. Always confirm specifics in the current terms before you deposit.

What counts as an inactive account

Operators typically define inactivity by the absence of certain actions over a stated period: no logins, no real-money hands, no tournament registrations, or no deposits and withdrawals. Some rooms count a login alone as activity, while others require actual play or a cashier transaction. The period itself can range from a few months to a year or longer, and some terms use different thresholds for different consequences.

That variation is why you cannot assume that your experience at one room carries over to another. A player who logs in monthly to check on promotions may be perfectly safe at one site and technically dormant at another. The only reliable approach is to read the section of the terms that covers inactive or dormant accounts, which is usually found in the general terms and conditions rather than the promotions page.

Common consequences of dormancy

Depending on the operator, a dormant account can face several different outcomes. Not every room applies all of them, and responsible operators send warnings before taking any step. Still, it helps to know what the typical menu looks like.

  • Monthly administration fees deducted from the balance until it reaches zero or the account is reactivated.
  • Account closure with a request to contact support in order to retrieve the remaining funds.
  • Bonus and ticket expiry, where unclaimed bonuses, tournament tickets and rewards simply lapse after their own deadlines.
  • Loyalty tier resets, which can lower your rakeback percentage or rewards status after a quiet period.
  • Additional verification before a withdrawal can be processed on a long-unused account.

The last item is the one players most often underestimate. If you return after a long absence and want to cash out, you may be asked to confirm your identity again, update an old payment method or verify a new wallet address. Our guide to KYC verification before cashing out is a good reminder that preparing documents early removes most of the friction.

Bonuses have their own clocks

Inactivity rules interact with bonus terms in ways that catch people out. Welcome offers on the Winning Poker Network and the Chico Network are released gradually as you generate rake, and each has a limited window to be cleared. If you stop playing partway through, the unreleased portion may expire when the window ends, regardless of whether your account has formally become dormant.

This is one more reason to deposit when you can actually play. Review the clearing period of the offer before you fund the account, and match it to your schedule. Our overview of poker bonus types explains how deposit matches, reload offers and rakeback differ, which helps you decide whether a particular promotion fits the amount of time you have available.

How crypto balances fit in

Many US and Canadian players fund offshore accounts with Bitcoin and other coins. Crypto deposits are convenient, but they also mean that if your account is closed or frozen, you need a valid withdrawal address and a clear process for getting funds back. Keep a record of the wallet you used, and avoid relying on an exchange deposit address that you may no longer control months later.

Price movement matters too. If your bankroll is parked in a volatile coin for a long period, the dollar value of the funds you eventually withdraw may differ considerably from what you deposited. Many players keep idle bankrolls in stablecoins or withdraw to their own wallet whenever they plan a break longer than a couple of months.

A simple routine to protect your bankroll

  1. Read the inactivity section of the terms before your first deposit and note the time thresholds in your calendar.
  2. If you plan a long break, withdraw the majority of your balance and leave only what you need for a quick return.
  3. Log in and play a small session or free tournament at regular intervals if the room counts that as activity.
  4. Keep your email address, phone number and payment details current so that warning messages and verification requests reach you.
  5. Save screenshots of relevant terms and balances so you can show support exactly what you saw.

If an account has already gone dormant

Do not panic and do not ignore it. Contact support through the official channel, explain that you want to reactivate the account or withdraw funds, and be ready to supply identification. Stay polite and factual, and keep a written record of the exchange. If the response is slow, escalate calmly using the steps described in our resources on dealing with support, rather than opening a new account that could conflict with the one-account-per-person rule.

It is also worth being realistic about the risk profile of offshore rooms. Because they operate outside the licensing regimes of individual US states and Canadian provinces, your recourse is mostly reputational. That is why checking a room's history, withdrawal reliability and clarity of terms matters more than any single bonus percentage.

Choosing rooms with player-friendly terms

When you compare operators, treat dormant account policy as one more line item beside rakeback, tournament schedules and banking options. Read the reviews of Americas Cardroom, BetOnline Poker and the other covered rooms, then check the live terms for yourself. Clear language, reasonable time windows and advance warnings are all signs of an operator that respects its players.

Frequently asked questions

Can a poker room charge fees on an inactive account?

Some operators do, usually after a stated period without logins or play. Always read the inactivity section of the current terms and conditions before depositing.

How do I avoid losing money to dormancy rules?

Withdraw most of your balance before a long break, note the inactivity thresholds, and keep your contact and payment details up to date so you receive warnings.
