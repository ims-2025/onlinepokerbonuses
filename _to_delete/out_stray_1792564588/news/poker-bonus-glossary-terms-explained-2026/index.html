Poker Bonus Glossary: Terms Every Player Should Know

Award Points, release rate, dealt rake, playthrough: a plain-English glossary of poker bonus terms so you can read any offer and know its true value.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 16, 2026·8 min read

Poker promotions come wrapped in jargon. One room advertises a match, another talks about release rates, a third highlights rakeback tiers and loyalty points. If you do not speak the language, it is easy to chase a headline number and end up with an offer that is worth far less than it looked. This glossary translates the terms you will meet most often at US- and Canada-facing rooms, with practical notes on how each one affects the real value of a deal. Use it alongside our best poker bonuses comparison.

Deposit and welcome bonus terms

Deposit match is the percentage of your deposit the room adds as bonus money. A 100% match up to $2,000 means a $2,000 deposit can earn a $2,000 bonus, though you will not receive it as cash on day one. Maximum bonus is the ceiling, and it matters because many players deposit more than they need just to reach it. Our guide on how much to deposit explains why the smartest deposit is often smaller than the cap.

Bonus code is a short text string entered at registration or in the cashier to activate a specific promotion. Miss it and the offer may not credit at all, which is one of the most common reasons for support tickets. Reload bonus is a smaller match on a later deposit, designed to keep existing players active. No-deposit bonus is a small credit or ticket given without funding, usually with strict limits on withdrawals.

Clearing and release terms

Most offshore poker bonuses are not paid as an instant lump sum. They are released in increments as you generate rake or tournament fees. Release rate or clearing rate describes how much play it takes to unlock each slice. On the Winning Poker Network, for example, Americas Cardroom releases roughly $1 of bonus per 27.5 Award Points earned. The lower the points needed per dollar, the faster you clear.

Award Points are the currency that tracks your activity. You earn them from rake paid in cash games and fees paid in tournaments, not from the money you win or lose. Playthrough or wagering requirement is the casino-style phrase for the same idea, and while poker rooms rarely use it in the same way, you will still see it in some terms. Expiry is the deadline to clear the bonus. If you miss it, any unreleased portion is usually forfeited, so check the clock before you claim.

Rake terms that decide your pace

Rake is the commission the room takes from cash-game pots. Tournament fee is the portion of a buy-in that goes to the house rather than the prize pool. Both feed your bonus clearing and your rakeback. Dealt rake versus contributed rake is a subtle but important split: under a dealt model you earn credit for hands you were dealt into, even if you folded, while a contributed model only credits rake based on your share of the pot. Read our breakdown before assuming two sites clear at the same speed.

Effective rake rate is a practical measure of how much rake you actually pay per hour, after rakeback and bonus value. It is the best way to compare rooms honestly, and it is covered in depth in our effective rake calculator guide.

Rakeback and loyalty terms

Rakeback returns a percentage of the rake you generate, win or lose. A room offering 30% rakeback is giving back about three out of every ten dollars of rake you contribute, which makes it one of the most reliable long-term edges in the game. Weekly rakeback pays on a fixed schedule, and instant rakeback credits as you play. VIP tiers or loyalty levels raise your rate as you generate more volume. You can see how these ladders differ between networks in our bonus types guide.

Leaderboards and rake races are competitions in which players earn prizes for ranking highly on points. Missions or challenges reward specific tasks, like winning a certain number of hands or playing a particular format. These are extras on top of the core bonus, and they are most valuable when you were going to play those games anyway.

Cashier and account terms

KYC stands for know your customer, the identity checks a room runs before approving withdrawals. Expect to submit a photo ID and proof of address, and do it early so cashouts are not delayed. Minimum deposit and minimum withdrawal set the floor on cashier transactions, and they vary by method. Deposit boost is an extra percentage added to deposits made with particular methods, often cryptocurrency. Withdrawal limits cap how much you can take out per transaction or per period.

Sister sites are rooms that share a network and player pool but run separate promotions, which sometimes lets a player claim two welcome offers while sitting at the same tables. Shared liquidity means player pools are combined across brands or jurisdictions. Overlay is the amount by which a tournament guarantee exceeds the actual buy-ins collected, which can make a tournament unusually valuable.

Terms that signal trouble

Some phrases deserve a second look before you accept an offer:

  • Bonus abuse clauses, which can void winnings if your play pattern looks like it was designed only to clear the bonus.
  • Restricted games, where certain formats contribute little or nothing toward clearing.
  • Sticky bonus, where the bonus itself cannot be withdrawn and only winnings are yours.
  • Short expiry windows, which can force you to play more than your bankroll or schedule allows.

None of these are automatically bad, but they change the math. Our guide to bonus terms red flags covers how to spot the clauses that cost players the most.

Putting the vocabulary to work

Once you know the language, you can reduce any offer to a few questions. How much will I need to deposit? How much play does it take to release each dollar? What is the deadline? Which games count? What rakeback or loyalty value comes on top? Answer those and you can rank any promotion fairly, without being swayed by a large headline number.

A worked example: suppose a room offers 100% up to $1,000 releasing at $1 per 30 points, and another offers 50% up to $1,000 releasing at $1 per 20 points with higher rakeback. The first looks twice as generous, yet the second may be easier to clear and more valuable overall for a moderate-volume player. The headline never tells that story; the underlying terms do.

Frequently asked questions

What does release rate mean in a poker bonus?

Release rate is how much rake or fee-generating play it takes to unlock each dollar of bonus. A lower number of points per dollar means you clear the bonus faster.

Is rakeback the same as a bonus?

No. A bonus is a limited-time offer, usually tied to a deposit, while rakeback is an ongoing return of a percentage of the rake you generate, win or lose.
