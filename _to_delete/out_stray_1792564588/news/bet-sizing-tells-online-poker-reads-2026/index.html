Bet Sizing Tells in Online Poker: What Opponents Reveal

Online players cannot see faces, but bet sizes and timing still leak information. Learn the most reliable sizing tells at low and mid stakes.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 16, 2026·8 min read

Live players hunt for shaking hands and nervous glances. Online, none of that exists, yet opponents still give away information on every street. The clues are numbers: how much they bet, how fast they act, and whether their sizes follow a pattern. Learning to read those signals is one of the cheapest edges available, especially at the stakes most bonus players start at. This guide covers the most dependable bet sizing tells, how to confirm them, and where they stop being reliable. Why sizing is information

Every bet size is a choice, and choices reveal intent. Strong players pick sizes deliberately, tying them to their range and the board. Weaker players pick sizes by feel, and feel tends to correlate with hand strength. They bet small when they are nervous or want a call, and big when they are afraid of being outdrawn or simply excited. Your job is not to assume a tell, but to collect evidence over many hands and test it against what you see at showdown.

The most important principle is to read sizing relative to the player, not in a vacuum. A pot-sized bet from one opponent may be a bluff, while the same bet from another is almost always the nuts. A tell is only useful once you have seen enough hands to know what that particular player means by it.

The small-bet tells

At low and micro stakes, a surprising number of players use tiny bets in two situations: when they are weak and want to see a cheap showdown, or when they are strong and hoping to induce action. Telling the two apart is the whole game. Start with context. A small bet on a dry flop from a player who raised preflop is often a standard continuation bet with a wide range. A small river bet after a long streak of passive action frequently signals a thin value hand or a blocking bet. Many recreational players will not fold those, so raising with a bluff is often a mistake, while calling with bluff-catchers is usually fine.

Compare that with the player who normally bets half pot or more and suddenly drops to a minimum bet. That deviation is the signal. It usually means the person wants to control the price of the hand, which points to a medium-strength holding rather than a monster or a pure bluff. For a deeper look at why small sizes work, see our guide on bet sizing fundamentals.

The overbet tells

Overbets, meaning bets larger than the pot, are rare at low stakes, and that rarity is itself a tell. In the player pools you will meet at most US and Canada facing rooms, an unexpected overbet is more likely to be value than a bluff, because bluffing with overbets requires a balanced strategy that most opponents have never studied. There are exceptions, notably regulars who use solvers and mix overbets deliberately. Check whether your opponent shows any other signs of study before assuming that rule applies.

Our full guide on overbet strategy covers when to use them yourself. For reads, the takeaway is simple: against passive or unknown opponents, treat a sudden jump to a huge bet on a scary board as strength until proven otherwise.

Timing tells that pair with size

Timing is the other half of the picture. Online, you can see how long an opponent takes to act, and the pattern across many hands is revealing. A quick small bet suggests a routine decision, while a long pause followed by a large raise can mean the person is working through a difficult spot or, in some cases, simulating difficulty. The clearest signal is a change from a player's baseline. If someone acts instantly all session and then takes a long time on one river decision, that is worth noting.

Be cautious with timing, because many factors distort it: multi-tabling, mobile play, distractions and internet lag. A slow action on a crowded schedule often means nothing. The most reliable use is to combine timing with sizing. A fast, minimum raise is typically automatic, while a slow, specific-sized bet more likely reflects thought, and thought usually follows a real decision rather than a default action.

How to build and test a read

Follow a simple process so you do not fool yourself:

  1. Record the observation: what size, which street, what board texture, and how fast.
  2. Wait for showdown evidence before treating it as a rule.
  3. Look for the pattern across at least several hands, not one dramatic story.
  4. Adjust the read if the opponent changes behavior or the sample contradicts it.

Even without software, a simple note such as small river bet equals weak showdown value is worth keeping in the room's built-in notes feature.

Where sizing tells stop working

Sizing tells are strongest against recreational players and weakest against regulars. Thoughtful opponents vary their sizes on purpose, and some use the same size for every hand type specifically so you cannot read them. If you notice a player using only one or two sizes regardless of the board, stop trying to extract information from the number and look to ranges and frequencies instead. You can read more about that approach in our piece on hand reading.

Also remember that your own sizing leaks information. If you always bet small with draws and large with value, observant opponents will figure it out. Use the same lens on yourself: review your sessions and ask what a stranger could infer from your pattern. Balancing your sizes slightly costs little and protects your reads from being turned against you.

Putting it together

Reading sizing is about accumulating small edges. A single tell rarely wins a pot on its own, but a handful of reasonably reliable ones, each tested against showdowns, will change your decisions in close spots. Combine them with solid fundamentals such as position, pot odds and sensible bankroll management, and your results over a large sample should improve noticeably.

Frequently asked questions

Are bet sizing tells reliable in online poker?

They are useful against recreational players and weaker against thoughtful regulars. Treat any tell as a hypothesis, confirm it with showdown evidence, and adjust if the opponent changes behavior.

Does action speed reveal hand strength online?

Sometimes, but timing is noisy because of multi-tabling and distractions. It is most reliable when an opponent deviates from their own baseline speed and when combined with bet size.
