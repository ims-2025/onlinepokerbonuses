Crypto Poker Deposits: Record-Keeping and Cost Basis Tips

Depositing crypto at poker sites creates records you may need later. Learn what to track, how cost basis works in general terms, and habits that help.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

Most players think about speed and fees when they pick a crypto coin for poker. Few think about paperwork. Yet every time you buy crypto, send it to a poker room, or cash out, you create a trail of transactions. Months later, when you want to know how much you actually won, or when tax season arrives, that trail either helps you or becomes a headache. A little organization at the start saves a great deal of effort later.

This guide covers what to record, how cost basis works in general terms, and habits that keep your numbers clean. It is educational and not tax or legal advice, and rules differ between the United States and Canada and can change, so confirm details with a qualified professional. If you are still choosing a coin, see our guide to the best crypto for poker deposits.

Why record-keeping matters for crypto poker players

A card deposit produces a statement. A crypto deposit produces a blockchain entry and whatever records your exchange or wallet keeps. Sending crypto to a poker room is not the same as depositing dollars, because the coin you hold may have gained or lost value between the day you bought it and the day you sent it. That difference can matter in many jurisdictions.

Beyond taxes, good records answer practical questions: Are you actually profitable? Which room pays fastest? Did that withdrawal arrive in full? Players who track everything tend to spot leaks in their banking just as they spot leaks in their game, which is the same logic behind reviewing your poker results.

What cost basis means in plain language

Cost basis is, roughly, what you paid to acquire an asset, including certain fees. If you bought a coin for a certain amount and later sent it to a poker room when it was worth more, many tax systems treat that disposal as a taxable event separate from your poker results. If it was worth less, you may have a loss. The details depend on where you live and how the authorities classify crypto, so this is a point to discuss with a professional rather than guess.

The key idea is that three things can matter at the same time: the price when you bought, the price when you deposited, and the price when you withdrew and sold or spent your winnings. Without records of each, reconstructing the numbers becomes slow and error-prone.

What to record for every transaction

You do not need fancy software to start. A spreadsheet works, as long as you are consistent. For each purchase, deposit, and withdrawal, capture the same core details.

  • Date and time of the transaction, ideally with the time zone.
  • The coin, the amount, and the fiat value at that moment.
  • Fees paid, whether exchange fees, network fees, or room fees.
  • The wallet addresses or room account involved, and the transaction ID so you can find it on the blockchain later.
  • The purpose: purchase, deposit, withdrawal, transfer between your own wallets, or conversion.

Save screenshots or exported statements from your exchange as well. Platforms can change their export tools or close accounts, and having your own copy protects you. Our notes on crypto network fees explain why fees deserve their own column.

Keep poker results separate from crypto price moves

One of the biggest benefits of good records is separating two different things: how you performed at the tables and how your crypto holdings moved. A player who wins a modest amount in poker but holds coins that lose half their value may feel like a loser, while the reverse can make a break-even player feel like a winner. Tracking the value of deposits and withdrawals in your home currency at the time of each transaction shows the truth.

Many players reduce this noise by using stablecoins for the bankroll, which keeps the dollar value steady. Our guide to stablecoin poker deposits explains the trade-offs. Note that even stablecoin transactions can generate records you should keep, so convenience does not remove the need for tracking.

Handling bonuses and promotions

Poker bonuses add another layer. A welcome bonus released in increments is usually credited as you pay rake, and the rules for how it is treated for tax purposes can vary by location. Record when each increment hit your account and the amount, along with the offer terms. Our explainer on whether poker bonuses are taxable covers the general debate in the United States and Canada, and a professional can tell you how it applies to you.

Keep a copy of the bonus terms as they stood when you accepted. Rooms can change promotions, and your own notes help if there is ever a dispute about what you were offered. If you are choosing an offer, our best poker bonuses page lists current options.

Simple habits that make life easier

The best system is the one you will actually follow. A few habits make a large difference over a year.

  • Use dedicated wallets for poker so personal and bankroll transactions do not mix.
  • Update your log weekly instead of waiting until year-end, when details are harder to recall.
  • Back up your records in at least two places, such as a cloud folder and an external drive.
  • Download exchange transaction histories regularly, not only when you need them.
  • Never record private keys or seed phrases in your tracking sheet. Our guide to crypto wallet security explains how to protect them.

When to talk to a professional

Tax treatment of crypto and gambling income is complex and varies by country and, in the United States, sometimes by state. Questions about how to classify winnings, how to report disposals, or how to handle losses are best answered by an accountant familiar with both crypto and gambling. Bring your organized records, and the conversation will be shorter, cheaper, and more accurate.

Start tracking before your next deposit

Good records are cheap to create and expensive to rebuild. Set up your log before you send your next coin, and treat every transaction as something you will want to explain later. Frequently asked questions

What records should I keep for crypto poker deposits?

Record the date, coin, amount, fiat value, fees, wallet or room account, and transaction ID for every purchase, deposit, and withdrawal, and save exported statements as backup.

Does cost basis apply when I send crypto to a poker site?

In many places, sending crypto can count as a disposal, so the price you paid versus its value at deposit may matter. Rules vary by country, so confirm details with a tax professional.
