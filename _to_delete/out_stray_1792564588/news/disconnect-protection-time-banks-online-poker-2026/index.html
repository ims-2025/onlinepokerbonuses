Disconnect Protection and Time Banks in Online Poker

Lost connection mid-hand? Learn how disconnect protection and time banks work at online poker rooms, and how to avoid costly timeouts in big pots.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 8, 2026·7 min read

Nothing sinks your stomach like watching the screen freeze right as the action reaches you in a huge pot. Internet connections drop, laptops sleep, and apps crash. Online poker rooms know this, which is why most software includes safeguards such as time banks and disconnect protection. They are rarely discussed when players compare sites, yet they can decide whether a bad connection costs you a few chips or an entire tournament run.

This guide explains how these protections typically work, how they differ between cash games and tournaments, and how to prepare so a technical hiccup does not become a financial loss. What a time bank is

A time bank is a reserve of extra seconds you can draw on when you need more than the standard decision clock. Most online rooms give you a base amount of time to act, often around 15 to 30 seconds, and then let you tap a button to add more when a tough spot arises. In tournaments, the bank frequently refreshes at certain intervals or levels, so a player who used it early still has some available later.

The exact amounts vary by room, game type, and speed. Turbo and hyper formats shorten the clock, while slower structures give more breathing room. The key habit is to check the rules in the lobby or help section of whichever room you use rather than assuming the settings match another site.

How disconnect protection works

Disconnect protection is the safety net that applies when your client loses contact with the server. Instead of immediately folding your hand, the software usually activates extra time, sometimes called a disconnect bank, so you have a chance to reconnect and act. The protection is generally limited, and when it runs out your hand is treated as folded or checked, depending on the situation.

In tournaments, a disconnected player may be placed in a sit-out state in which blinds and antes are still paid, but the player folds hands automatically when action reaches them. In cash games, the seat can be held for a limited period before the player is removed from the table. Policies differ, and not every room is generous about protection in cash games.

All-in protection

One of the most valuable features is all-in protection. If you are already all-in and your connection drops, the hand normally plays out to completion, because no further decisions are required. The harder cases are the ones where you disconnect while facing a bet. Some rooms treat that as a fold after the clock expires. Others may give a short grace window. Either way, you cannot assume that a dropped connection will be forgiven.

Many serious players treat this as a reason to avoid pressing the all-in button with a weak connection. If your Wi-Fi is unreliable, avoid playing long tournament sessions or deep runs on it until you have a more stable setup.

Tournament versus cash game rules

Tournaments are the highest-stakes environment for technical problems. A single missed decision on the bubble or at a final table can cost a player thousands. As a result, tournament software tends to include stronger protection, including longer time banks and automatic sit-out handling. The best-known multi-table events on US- and Canada-facing sites, such as the Venom and other major series, draw players from many connection types, so robust protection matters.

Cash games, by contrast, rely more on the premise that you can rebuy and move on. Protection is lighter, and tables move at a fast pace. If you play fast-fold formats, the speed is even greater, and dropping out mid-hand can mean an automatic fold and a lost opportunity. Fair play and abuse

Disconnect protection exists for honest technical problems, not for gaining an edge. Some players try to abuse it, deliberately disconnecting to avoid tough decisions or to stall. Reputable rooms track repeated patterns of disconnects, and accounts that appear to exploit the system can be warned, restricted, or closed. That risk is not worth taking, and it can interfere with cashouts if the room later reviews your account, as discussed in our piece on how rooms detect bonus abuse.

Practical ways to protect yourself

You cannot control your internet provider, but you can reduce the odds of a costly drop. These habits are inexpensive and effective.

  • Use a wired Ethernet connection for important tournaments whenever possible, since it is steadier than Wi-Fi.
  • Close bandwidth-hungry apps such as streaming, large downloads, and cloud backups before you register.
  • Keep your device plugged in and disable automatic sleep so the screen does not lock mid-hand.
  • Know where your time bank button is and use it in big decisions instead of rushing.
  • Have a backup connection ready, such as a phone hotspot, for deep runs in major events.
  • Install the latest version of the poker client and restart it before a long session.
  • Read the room's disconnect policy in the help section so you know what happens when protection runs out.

Mobile players should be especially careful. Switching from Wi-Fi to cellular data can interrupt a session, and notifications can bump you out of the app at the wrong moment. What to do after a bad disconnect

If a technical issue costs you chips, contact support quickly with specifics. Provide the table name, tournament ID, time, and hand number, and describe what happened. Rooms generally review logs and can see whether the problem was on their side or yours. Do not expect refunds for problems that stem from your own connection, but if the server failed, a good operator will often make it right.

It is also wise to document the incident. Take screenshots, note the time, and save any chat messages. A calm, factual complaint is taken far more seriously than an angry one, and it creates a record in case you need to escalate. Why software quality matters when choosing a room

Most players choose a poker room because of its bonuses, traffic, and banking options. Those matter a great deal. But software stability, reconnect behavior, and the quality of time bank and disconnect settings deserve a place on the checklist too, especially if you play tournaments or long sessions. Frequently asked questions

What happens if I disconnect during an online poker hand?

Most rooms activate extra time or a disconnect bank so you can reconnect. If it runs out, your hand is usually folded or checked, though a hand where you are already all-in plays out automatically.

Does a time bank refresh in tournaments?

Many online tournaments replenish the time bank at set intervals or blind levels, but the exact amounts differ by room and format. Check the help section of your poker room to confirm its rules.
