Effective Rake Rate: Compare Poker Bonuses and Rakeback

Headline bonus percentages hide the real cost of playing. Learn to calculate your effective rake rate with bonuses and rakeback to compare poker rooms.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 10, 2026·8 min read

Every poker room advertises its offers in the loudest terms available: big match percentages, large maximum bonuses, shiny rakeback numbers. The trouble is that those headline figures are almost impossible to compare directly. One room pays a bonus on a deposit over time, another pays rakeback weekly, a third offers tournament tickets. Which one actually costs you the least to play? The tool that answers this is your effective rake rate, and once you learn to calculate it, the comparison gets much simpler.

This guide walks through what effective rake means, how to calculate it with a simple formula, and how to use it alongside the information on our best poker bonuses page. The numbers in the examples are illustrative so you can follow the math; always check the live terms at the room before you deposit.

What effective rake actually measures

Rake is the fee the room takes from pots or tournament buy-ins. Your gross rake is simply what you pay over a period of play. Your effective rake is what you pay after subtracting everything the room returns to you: bonus money you earn, rakeback, cash prizes from promotions and leaderboards, and even rewards you can convert to cash.

Put another way, effective rake is your real cost of playing. Two rooms with identical rake structures can have very different effective rake once one of them pays back thirty percent and the other pays back ten. And a room with slightly higher rake can still win if its returns are strong enough. Looking at effective rake turns a confusing pile of promotions into a single comparable number.

The basic formula

The calculation is straightforward. Over any period you want to measure, such as a month, take:

  1. Gross rake paid: the total rake or tournament fees you generated.
  2. Total returns: bonus cash released, rakeback paid, and any cash value from promotions you actually earned.
  3. Effective rake = Gross rake minus Total returns.
  4. Effective rake rate = Effective rake divided by Gross rake.

Imagine you generate 500 dollars in rake in a month. You earn 100 dollars in released bonus money and 75 dollars in rakeback. Your total returns are 175 dollars, your effective rake is 325 dollars, and your effective rake rate is 65 percent of gross. In other words, you got back 35 percent. That 35 percent is the number to compare across rooms.

Why bonus percentages mislead

A 100 percent match sounds like it doubles your money. It does not. The bonus is released as you generate rake, usually in small increments, and you only receive it if you play enough volume. If you deposit 1,000 dollars and the bonus releases at a fixed ratio of rake, whether you clear it depends on how much you play. Players who clear only half effectively got a 50 percent smaller bonus.

That is why the metric that matters is not the headline size but the return per dollar of rake. A bonus that requires a modest amount of rake to unlock gives a higher return per dollar than a larger one that demands far more play. We explore this in more depth in our article on why big match percentages can mislead, and the how to claim guide explains the mechanics of release schedules.

Adding rakeback to the picture

Rakeback is the cleanest return to model because it is usually a fixed percentage of rake generated. A room offering 30 percent rakeback returns 30 cents on every dollar of rake, from the first hand. That is easy to put into the formula.

Bonuses are trickier because they depend on your volume. If you are a low-volume player who will not clear a large bonus, rakeback or a smaller bonus with a fast release may produce a better effective rake. If you are a high-volume grinder, a large bonus can be superb because you will clear all of it. Your own volume is the most important input, which is why it pays to keep records. Our guide to tracking poker results shows what to log.

  • Low volume (casual players): favor flat rakeback and small, quickly released bonuses.
  • Medium volume: a mix of a mid-sized bonus and ongoing rakeback usually wins.
  • High volume: large bonuses plus tier-based loyalty rewards can bring the biggest returns.

Worked comparison of two offers

Suppose Room A pays a bonus that returns 20 percent of your rake as you clear it, plus 10 percent weekly rakeback. Room B pays no big bonus but offers a flat 27 percent rakeback. On the surface Room A looks bigger because of the two layers. Add them: 20 plus 10 gives 30 percent, but only if you fully clear the bonus within its expiry. If you clear only half the bonus, Room A's return drops to 10 plus 10, or 20 percent, and Room B's 27 percent wins easily.

The lesson is to run the calculation twice: once assuming you clear everything, and once assuming you clear only a realistic portion. If an offer is only competitive in the best case, treat it with caution. Reading the fine print on expiry windows is part of this, and our review pages for BetOnline Poker and Americas Cardroom outline how their programs are structured.

Don't forget the hidden costs

Effective rake focuses on returns, but a few other costs should be in your mental ledger. Crypto network fees on deposits and withdrawals, currency conversion if you play from Canada and bank in Canadian dollars, and any withdrawal fees all reduce your net result. They do not change the rake, but they change what you keep.

Also consider game quality. A room with a slightly worse effective rake but much softer games can be the more profitable place to play, because your win rate depends on opponents as well as costs. Bonus value is one factor in choosing a room, not the only one.

A simple monthly routine

You do not need a spreadsheet empire. At the end of each month, write down your gross rake, the bonus cash and rakeback you received, and calculate the percentage returned. Over a few months, patterns emerge: you will see which rooms give you the best return at your volume and which promotions you consistently fail to finish. Use that data to decide where to concentrate play.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good effective rake rate?

There is no universal number, but the lower your effective rake as a share of gross rake, the better. Many players aim to have 25 to 40 percent of their rake returned through bonuses and rakeback.

Should I choose rakeback or a big deposit bonus?

Choose by volume. Casual players usually do better with steady rakeback, while high-volume players who will clear the whole offer can profit from a large deposit bonus.
