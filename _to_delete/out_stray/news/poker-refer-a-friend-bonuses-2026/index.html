Refer-a-Friend Poker Bonuses: What They Really Pay

Referral offers promise easy money for inviting friends, but the payout is tied to their rake. Here is how these bonuses actually work and when they pay.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 27, 2026·9 min read

Almost every online poker room runs some version of a referral programme, and almost every one of them is advertised in the same optimistic register: invite a friend, both of you get paid. It sounds like the rarest thing in gambling — free money that costs nobody anything. In practice a referral bonus is one of the more conditional offers a room will hand you, and understanding the condition is the difference between a promotion that quietly pays you for years and one that never pays a cent.

The mechanics are worth learning because referral programmes sit oddly in the bonus landscape. They are not a deposit match and not really rakeback, but they borrow the release logic of both. If you have already worked through our best poker bonuses comparisons and know how a welcome offer clears, most of that intuition transfers directly — with one important twist, which is that the volume requirement is being met by somebody who is not you.

What a referral bonus actually is

Strip away the marketing and a refer-a-friend offer is a revenue share on somebody else's rake. The room gives you a unique link or code. When a new player signs up through it, deposits and starts playing, the room credits you a percentage of the rake that player generates — sometimes as a one-off payment once they cross a threshold, sometimes as an ongoing trickle for as long as they keep playing.

That single structural fact explains everything else about these promotions. The room is not being generous; it is outsourcing customer acquisition to you and paying you a slice of the resulting revenue, exactly as it pays an affiliate. Your friend is worth something to the operator only if they play, so you are worth something to the operator only if your friend plays. No play, no payment, regardless of how many people you sign up.

The two payment structures, and why the difference matters

Referral offers generally fall into one of two shapes, and they suit completely different situations.

  • One-off milestone payments. You receive a fixed amount once your referred player has deposited and generated a set quantity of rake or points. Simple, capped, and fast — but the relationship ends there. Once the milestone is hit, the referral stops earning.
  • Ongoing revenue share. You receive a percentage of your friend's rake indefinitely, or for a defined period such as their first year. Slower to start, no ceiling, and vastly more valuable if the person you referred turns into a regular.

For most players the milestone structure is the realistic one, because most referred friends play casually for a few weeks and then drift. The ongoing structure only outperforms if you refer someone who genuinely sticks — and you cannot know that in advance. If a room offers both and lets you choose, the honest question is not which pays more in theory but how likely the specific person you are inviting is to still be playing in six months.

There is also a common third element: many rooms pay the referred player a bonus too, typically an enhanced version of the standard welcome offer. That part is worth checking carefully, because in some cases the referral welcome bonus is smaller than the room's public promotion. Sending a friend through your link so you get paid, while costing them a better deposit match, is a poor trade for the friendship.

The conditions that stop referral bonuses from paying

Referral terms tend to be longer and stricter than deposit-match terms, for the obvious reason that the format invites abuse. The clauses that most often bite honest players are these.

  1. Same-household and shared-IP restrictions. Almost universally, you cannot refer somebody who plays from your address or your network. Rooms enforce this aggressively because self-referral is the most obvious way to game the system, and the enforcement does not distinguish between a fraudster and a genuine housemate.
  2. Rake thresholds before anything releases. A milestone payment usually requires a meaningful amount of rake from the referred player. A friend who deposits, plays four sessions and quits will typically not reach it.
  3. Time windows. Some programmes require the threshold to be met within a set period from signup. Slow, casual play can fall outside it even if the total eventually arrives.
  4. Link attribution. If the friend registers without the code, or clears cookies, or signs up on a different device, the referral may not attach at all. Attribution failures are the single most common reason a referral silently pays nothing.
  5. Account verification. Both accounts generally need to be fully verified before any referral credit is released. Our walkthrough of how to claim a poker bonus covers the same verification mechanics that apply here.

None of these are hidden — they are in the terms — but they are rarely in the banner, and the gap between the banner and the terms is where the value of the promotion actually lives.

How referral value compares to the alternatives

Set against the other ways a poker room pays you, referral income is unusual: it has no volume requirement on your part, which makes it the only bonus you can earn while not playing. That sounds like a strong argument for it, and for a player with a genuinely large poker-playing social circle it is. For everybody else the numbers are modest.

The arithmetic is straightforward. Your payout is a percentage of a friend's rake, and a casual recreational player generates a fairly small amount of rake in a month. A percentage of a small number is a smaller number. Compare that against your own ongoing rakeback, which is a percentage of your own volume and entirely under your control, and it becomes clear which of the two is the reliable income stream. Referral money is a supplement, not a strategy.

Where it does become meaningful is in aggregate and over time. Someone who introduces a handful of friends to the same room over a couple of years, a few of whom become regulars, can end up with a steady trickle that outlasts every welcome bonus they ever cleared. That is the realistic best case, and it takes years rather than weeks. Network differences: WPN and Chico

Referral structures vary between the two networks that serve most US and Canadian players, and the variation tends to follow the same pattern as their broader bonus philosophy. Rooms on both networks have historically run referral schemes, but terms, thresholds and whether the payment is one-off or recurring have changed repeatedly over the years — as has whether a given room promotes the offer publicly at all.

That volatility is the practical point. Referral terms are among the most frequently revised promotions a room runs, because they are the ones most exposed to abuse. Anything you read about a specific rate, threshold or payment schedule should be verified against the room's current promotions page before you send a single invitation, not after. Using a referral offer sensibly

The sane approach is to treat referral bonuses as something you collect rather than something you chase. If you already play at a room, already like it, and a friend already asked you where to play — send the link. That costs you nothing and may pay you something. What does not work is the reverse: recruiting people into poker because a promotion pays you for it. That is a good way to damage friendships and a bad way to make money, since the payout depends entirely on them losing rake for months.

Check three things before you send anything. First, that the friend gets at least as good a welcome offer through your link as they would without it. Second, that neither of you is caught by a household or shared-network clause. Third, that the attribution mechanism is a code they can type rather than a cookie that might expire — codes survive device switches, cookies frequently do not.

Do that and a referral bonus becomes what it should be: a small, occasional, zero-effort addition to your poker income that sits alongside the offers you are actually optimising. Frequently asked questions

Do refer-a-friend poker bonuses pay instantly?

Almost never. Most programmes require your referred player to deposit, verify their account and generate a set amount of rake before anything is credited, and some pay as an ongoing share rather than a lump sum.

Can I refer someone who lives in my house?

Usually not. Nearly all referral terms exclude players sharing an address, device or IP address, and rooms enforce these clauses strictly because self-referral is the most common form of abuse.
