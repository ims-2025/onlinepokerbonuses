Playing Online Poker While Traveling: The State Rules

Crossing a state line can lock you out of your poker account. Here is how geolocation works, what happens to your funds and how to plan around it.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 27, 2026·10 min read

One of the least intuitive things about online poker in North America is that your account's usefulness can change depending on where you are standing. A player with a funded balance and a bonus halfway cleared can drive two hours, open the client in a hotel room and find themselves locked out — not because anything is wrong with the account, but because the software has worked out that they are no longer in a place where the operator is licensed to serve them.

This trips people up constantly, partly because the rules differ enormously depending on which kind of site you play at. A regulated state-licensed room and an offshore room behave completely differently when you travel, and the difference is worth understanding before you pack rather than after you land. Our US poker guide sets out the broader legal picture; this piece deals specifically with what changes when you move.

Why your location matters at all

In the United States, online poker is licensed at state level rather than federally. A regulated operator holds a licence from a specific state's gaming regulator, and that licence permits it to accept wagers from people physically located inside that state's borders at the moment they play. Residency is not the test. A lifelong resident of a regulated state who is sitting in an airport three states away is, for licensing purposes, not eligible to play.

Because the obligation is on the operator rather than the player, enforcement happens in software. Regulated sites run geolocation checks continuously — at login, before you are seated, and periodically during play. If the check cannot confirm you are inside the licensed boundary, the client stops you.

Offshore rooms operate on a different footing entirely. They are licensed outside the United States and do not run state-boundary geolocation, which is why the same account behaves consistently whether you are at home or on the road. That consistency is one of the practical reasons offshore rooms remain popular with players who travel, and it is covered in more depth alongside the trade-offs in our bonus types and review pages.

How geolocation actually works on regulated sites

The technology is more thorough than most players assume. It does not simply read your IP address — IP-based location is easy to spoof and easy to get wrong, so regulated operators layer several signals together.

  • Wi-Fi network triangulation. The client examines nearby wireless networks and compares them against a mapped database of known access points. This is the primary desktop signal and it is accurate to a surprisingly small radius.
  • Mobile device GPS. On phones and tablets the app requests location permission directly and reads the device's own positioning.
  • Cellular tower data. On mobile networks, tower identity provides a cross-check against GPS.
  • IP address and network analysis. Used mainly as a consistency check and to detect proxies, VPNs and other location-masking tools.
  • Proximity to borders. Near a state line the checks tighten, because the difference between legal and illegal can be a few hundred metres.

A failed check is usually not permanent. The most common causes are mundane: location permissions switched off, a VPN left running from work, a corporate network routing traffic through another state, or a device that has not been given time to acquire a proper fix. Turning off the VPN, enabling location services and reconnecting resolves most failures.

The VPN question, answered plainly

Players ask constantly whether a VPN solves the travel problem. The short answer is no, and attempting it is a bad idea for reasons that have nothing to do with whether it technically works.

First, it mostly does not work. Regulated geolocation stacks are built specifically to detect location-masking, and a VPN that changes your IP while your Wi-Fi environment and GPS say something different is a clear signal rather than a disguise. Second, and far more importantly, using one is a terms-of-service violation at essentially every operator, regulated and offshore alike. The consequence is not a warning. Accounts are routinely closed, and closure typically means forfeited bonuses, forfeited promotional balances and a withdrawal process that becomes very slow and very documentation-heavy.

The asymmetry here is stark: the upside is a few sessions you would otherwise miss, and the downside is your balance, your cleared bonus and your account history at that room. There is no version of that trade that makes sense, and it is worth being explicit that this is not a cautious hedge — it is the single fastest way to lose access to money you have already earned.

What happens to your money and your bonus

Being unable to play is not the same as being unable to access your account. On regulated sites, cashier functions generally remain available even when play is blocked, so you can usually withdraw or check your balance from out of state. Your funds are not frozen or at risk; they are simply not playable until you return.

The bonus side is where travel genuinely costs you. Deposit bonuses release against rake and almost always carry an expiry window, and a fortnight away is a fortnight of that window elapsing with no volume generated. If you know you are travelling, the sensible move is to time your deposit around the trip rather than through it. Our how to claim walkthrough covers reading the expiry terms before you commit, which is exactly the number that matters here.

The same applies to loyalty and rake-race periods that reset weekly or monthly. A week away can drop you out of a tier you have been maintaining, and depending on the programme, regaining it can take longer than losing it did.

Crossing into Canada, and back

International travel adds a layer. Canada regulates online gambling provincially, with Ontario operating an open licensed market and other provinces running their own arrangements. A US-licensed regulated account will not function north of the border, and Ontario-licensed accounts do not function outside Ontario.

Offshore rooms are again the more portable option, since a single account generally works across both countries — though the practical details of banking, currency and available promotions can differ. Our Canada poker guide covers which rooms serve Canadian players and how the provincial picture fits together.

One thing worth flagging for anyone crossing borders regularly: tax treatment of poker winnings differs between the two countries and is not determined by where you happened to be sitting when you played. That is a question for an accountant rather than a poker site, but it is worth asking one if travel is a regular part of your poker life.

Planning around it

The practical checklist before a trip is short. Check whether your room is state-licensed or offshore, because that single fact determines whether you can play at all. Check the expiry date on any active bonus against your return date. Withdraw or move funds if you will not be back before something lapses. Make sure your mobile app has location permissions enabled before you leave, so a denied permission does not look like an attempted evasion when you first try to log in from somewhere new.

And if you are choosing where to play in the first place and travel is a regular feature of your life, weight portability accordingly — an account that works everywhere you go is worth more than a marginally larger welcome offer that works in one state. Frequently asked questions

Can I play on a regulated US poker site while visiting another state?

No. State-licensed sites are permitted to accept play only from inside the licensed state's borders, and geolocation software checks your physical location continuously. Your account and funds remain accessible, but play is blocked.

Will a VPN let me keep playing while I travel?

It generally will not work, and it violates the terms of service at essentially every poker room. Accounts detected using location-masking tools are commonly closed with bonuses and promotional balances forfeited.
