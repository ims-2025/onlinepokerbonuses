Are Poker Bonuses Taxable? US & Canada Overview

Do you owe tax on a poker welcome bonus or rakeback? Here's a plain-language overview of how winnings and bonuses are generally treated in the US and

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 9, 2026·8 min read

It's one of the most common questions new players ask after they claim their first offer: do I owe tax on this? Whether it's a welcome bonus, released rakeback, or an outright tournament score, the tax treatment of online poker money is a genuine point of confusion — and it works very differently depending on which side of the US–Canada border you sit on. This article is a plain-language overview of the general principles, not personal tax advice. Tax rules are detailed, they change, and everyone's situation is different, so treat what follows as a starting point for a conversation with a qualified professional, not a substitute for one.

Before anything else, one honest caveat: we're a poker-bonus site, not accountants or attorneys. Nothing here should be read as a definitive ruling on your personal liability. If real money is involved, the right move is always to consult a licensed tax professional in your own jurisdiction. With that said, understanding the broad framework helps you ask the right questions — and it starts with a simple distinction between the two countries.

The United States: winnings are generally taxable income

In the US, the default position is straightforward in principle: gambling winnings, including poker winnings, are generally treated as taxable income at the federal level. That framework doesn't care whether you played in a licensed cardroom, a home game, or an offshore online room — the income is the income. Recreational players typically report winnings as 'other income,' and the tax code has long-standing rules about how losses can be deducted, which generally allow gambling losses to offset winnings only up to the amount of those winnings, and typically only if you itemize.

Where does a welcome bonus fit into that? Bonuses and rakeback are a grayer area than a clean tournament cash, but the conservative, widely-held view is that money you actually receive and can withdraw is best treated as reportable. A bonus that has cleared into withdrawable balance, or rakeback that has been paid to your account, looks a lot more like realized income than a pending promotional credit you haven't unlocked yet. Because these promotional mechanics don't map neatly onto simple 'winnings' language, this is exactly the kind of detail worth confirming with a professional.

  • US federal treatment generally counts gambling winnings as taxable income.
  • Losses can typically offset winnings only up to the amount won, and usually only if you itemize.
  • State-level rules vary widely and sit on top of federal obligations.
  • Serious, professional players may face different treatment than recreational ones.

State taxes add another layer. Individual states set their own rules, and some tax gambling income while others structure it differently, so two players with identical results can owe different amounts depending on where they live. Canada: a very different starting point

Canada approaches the question from an almost opposite direction. For the typical recreational player, gambling winnings — including poker winnings — have generally not been treated as taxable income. The long-standing principle is that windfalls and the proceeds of a hobby or pastime fall outside the definition of taxable income, which is why the average Canadian recreational player has historically not owed tax on a lucky tournament run or a cleared bonus.

The important nuance is the word 'recreational.' The picture can change for someone whose poker play rises to the level of a business — a genuine professional playing with the systematic, profit-seeking regularity of a commercial operation. In those cases the analysis is more involved, because income from a business is treated differently from a windfall. Where the line falls between a serious hobbyist and a professional is a fact-specific question that has generated real debate, and it's precisely the kind of situation where professional advice is essential rather than optional.

There's also the matter of what happens to the money after you win it. Even in a country where the winnings themselves may not be taxed for a recreational player, investment income earned on those funds once they're sitting in an account can be a separate matter. Why bonuses and rakeback are the trickiest piece

Welcome bonuses and rakeback don't behave like a normal pot you won at the table, and that's what makes them the murkiest corner of the whole topic. A welcome bonus starts life as a pending promotional credit that only becomes real money as you generate rake — a rebate you earn back through play, as our bonus types guide explains. Until it clears, there's a reasonable argument it isn't income at all, because you can't withdraw it. Once it clears into a withdrawable balance, the case for treating it as realized value gets stronger.

Rakeback sits in a similar spot: it's money paid back to you based on the rake you contributed, arriving as a credit or cash payment. Reasonable people can frame it as a rebate or as income depending on the jurisdiction and the specifics, which is exactly why a blanket answer doesn't exist. The practical takeaway is not to guess — it's to keep good records and let a professional apply the rules to your actual numbers.

Keep records regardless of where you live

Whatever your country, the single most useful habit is documentation. Tax questions are far easier to answer — and any obligation far easier to meet — when you can show exactly what came in and when. Good record-keeping also protects you: if a question ever arises, a clear paper trail is worth more than the best memory.

  • Log deposits, withdrawals, and dates so you can reconstruct your year if needed.
  • Keep track of bonus amounts as they clear and rakeback as it's paid, not just net results.
  • Save cashier statements and transaction histories from each room while you have access.
  • If you use crypto to move funds, note the value at the time of each transaction as well.

That last point matters more than players expect. If you deposit or cash out in Bitcoin or another cryptocurrency, the value of that crypto at the moment of each transaction can itself carry tax implications separate from the poker results — another reason clean records and professional guidance are worth the effort.

The bottom line

The short version: in the US, poker winnings are generally taxable income, and money that has actually cleared or been paid to you is best treated conservatively; in Canada, the typical recreational player has generally not owed tax on winnings, though professionals and edge cases are a different story. Bonuses and rakeback are the trickiest category in both countries because they don't map cleanly onto simple 'winnings' language, which makes personalized advice especially valuable.

None of this should stop you from claiming a strong offer — it should just prompt you to keep records and ask a professional the right questions. Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay tax on a poker welcome bonus?

It depends on your country and situation. This is general information, not advice — confirm your own position with a qualified tax professional.

Yes, quite differently. US federal rules generally treat gambling winnings as taxable income, while Canada has generally not taxed a typical recreational player's winnings. Professional players and specific circumstances can change the analysis in both countries.
