Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash Poker Deposits 2026

Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash sit on most poker cashier menus but few players use them. Here is where each one actually beats Bitcoin and Litecoin.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published September 22, 2026·8 min read

Open the cashier at almost any US-facing poker room and you will find a longer crypto list than you expected. Bitcoin and Litecoin sit at the top because that is where the volume is. Further down, usually unremarked, sit Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash — two of the oldest altcoins still in wide circulation, supported by most rooms and used by almost nobody.

That gap between availability and usage is worth examining. Neither coin is the obvious default, but each has a specific situation where it is the best option on the menu, and knowing which situation that is can save you time and fees. This guide covers how both behave for poker deposits and withdrawals, and where they fit alongside the coins covered in our crypto banking guides.

Why rooms support them at all

Poker rooms add coins for two reasons: their payment processor already supports them, and players occasionally ask. Neither Dogecoin nor Bitcoin Cash requires bespoke integration — both are Bitcoin-derived chains with mature infrastructure and long uptime records, which makes them cheap for a processor to carry.

That is also why support is near-universal. If a room takes Bitcoin, it almost certainly takes Bitcoin Cash, because the address handling and confirmation logic are nearly identical. Dogecoin follows the same pattern through the Litecoin-adjacent codebase it shares heritage with. The practical upshot is that these coins are usually available at Americas Cardroom, BetOnline Poker and the other main US-facing rooms without you needing to ask support.

Dogecoin for poker deposits

Dogecoin's reputation as a joke coin obscures what it actually is from a payments perspective: a fast, extremely cheap, high-throughput chain with a one-minute block target and no meaningful congestion history.

The case for it. Transaction fees are among the lowest of any coin a poker room accepts — typically a fraction of a cent to a few cents, regardless of how much you are sending. Blocks arrive roughly every minute, so the three to six confirmations most rooms require clear in a handful of minutes rather than the half hour Bitcoin can take. For a player moving small amounts frequently, that combination is genuinely hard to beat.

The case against it. Dogecoin is inflationary by design — a fixed number of new coins enters circulation every year, forever — and it is unusually sensitive to sentiment-driven price swings. Holding a poker bankroll in Dogecoin between sessions exposes you to volatility that has nothing to do with poker. It is a transfer rail, not a store of value.

  • Best for — small to mid-size deposits where speed and fee cost matter more than anything else.
  • Confirmation speed — typically minutes, with roughly one-minute blocks.
  • Fees — negligible, and largely independent of transfer size.
  • Worst for — parking a bankroll on-chain or in an exchange account for weeks at a time.
  • Watch for — minimum deposit thresholds set in dollar terms, which move as the price does.

Bitcoin Cash for poker deposits

Bitcoin Cash forked from Bitcoin in 2017 over the block-size debate, and the result is a chain with the same security model and a far larger capacity. In practice that means Bitcoin-like handling with fees that stay low even when the wider crypto market is busy.

The case for it. Fees are consistently low — usually under a cent — and, crucially, they stay low. Bitcoin's fee market spikes when the network is congested, which is exactly when you are least likely to want to wait. Bitcoin Cash has enough spare block capacity that congestion pricing effectively does not happen. It also shares Bitcoin's ten-minute block target, so confirmation times are predictable rather than fast.

The case against it. Ten-minute blocks mean a room requiring three confirmations will take around half an hour, which is slower than Litecoin or Dogecoin. Liquidity is thinner than Bitcoin's, so exchange spreads can be slightly wider. And the address format catches people out — Bitcoin Cash uses a distinct address scheme, and sending BCH to a Bitcoin address or vice versa is a classic and usually unrecoverable error.

That last point deserves emphasis. Always copy the deposit address directly from the cashier and confirm the coin selection on both ends before sending. The most expensive crypto mistakes in poker are not price swings — they are transfers sent on the wrong chain. How they stack up against the defaults

Most players default to Bitcoin or Litecoin, and for good reason. Here is where Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash actually win.

  1. Against Bitcoin — both win decisively on fees, and Dogecoin wins on speed. Bitcoin's advantage is liquidity and the fact that it is the coin every bonus promotion is denominated around. If a room advertises a crypto deposit boost, it is usually Bitcoin-first.
  2. Against Litecoin — this is the closer contest. Litecoin has 2.5-minute blocks and low fees, which makes it the all-round pick for most players. Dogecoin is faster still and marginally cheaper; Bitcoin Cash is comparable on fee but slower on confirmations.
  3. Against stablecoins — stablecoins avoid the price-swing problem entirely, which is a real advantage for a bankroll. But network fees depend heavily on which chain you use, and some routes are far more expensive than Dogecoin or Bitcoin Cash.

The honest summary: Litecoin remains the sensible default for most US poker players, Dogecoin is the better choice when you want the fastest and cheapest possible small transfer, and Bitcoin Cash is the better choice when the Bitcoin network is busy and you want Bitcoin-style handling without the fee spike.

Bonus eligibility — read this before you deposit

This is the part that actually costs players money. Crypto welcome bonuses and deposit boosts are frequently coin-specific, and the terms do not always spell that out on the promotional page.

Some rooms apply their crypto bonus to any supported coin. Others limit the enhanced percentage to Bitcoin only, or to Bitcoin and Litecoin, with other coins treated as standard deposits at the base rate. Depositing in Dogecoin when the promotion required Bitcoin means you get the smaller bonus with no way to retroactively fix it.

Check the specific terms before depositing, not after. The relevant line is usually in the promotion's terms rather than its headline, and support will confirm it in a two-minute chat if the wording is ambiguous. Withdrawals and the round-trip problem

Deposit choice usually determines withdrawal choice. Most rooms will pay you out in the coin you deposited with, and some enforce that strictly as an anti-fraud measure. So the question is not just which coin is best to send, but which is best to receive and then convert.

Here Dogecoin's volatility becomes relevant in a way it is not on deposit. A deposit is in and converted to site dollars within minutes. A withdrawal sits in your wallet until you move it to an exchange and sell — and if that takes a few days, the price you actually realise may differ noticeably from the price when the room sent it.

The practical mitigation is simple: convert promptly, or use a coin whose price you are comfortable holding. Bitcoin Cash is less jumpy than Dogecoin but still a volatile asset. If you want a genuinely stable round trip, a stablecoin is the tool for that job, not either of these coins.

The bottom line

Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash are legitimate, well-supported options that most poker players overlook for reasons of reputation rather than performance. Dogecoin is the fastest and cheapest rail on most cashier menus. Bitcoin Cash is the congestion-proof Bitcoin alternative. Neither is where you should keep a bankroll, and both need a bonus-terms check before you send anything.

Frequently asked questions

Do poker sites accept Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash?

Most US-facing poker rooms support both, since each is easy for payment processors to carry. Availability is near-universal even though usage is low.

Which is cheaper for poker deposits, Dogecoin or Bitcoin Cash?

Both are very cheap, but Dogecoin is typically faster thanks to roughly one-minute blocks versus Bitcoin Cash's ten-minute target.
