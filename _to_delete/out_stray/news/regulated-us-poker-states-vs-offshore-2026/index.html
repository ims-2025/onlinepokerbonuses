Regulated US Poker States vs Offshore Rooms in 2026

A handful of US states run regulated online poker. Here is how those rooms compare with offshore sites on traffic, bonus value and cashout speed.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 17, 2026·9 min read

Ask two American poker players where they play and you will often get two completely different answers. One will name a state-licensed site operating under a gaming commission; the other will name an offshore room on the Winning Poker Network or Chico. Both are playing legally from their own perspective, both think the other has made the wrong choice, and both are partly right. The honest comparison is more nuanced than either camp usually admits — and it starts with understanding what regulated actually means. Our USA poker guide covers the state-by-state picture; this article digs into the practical trade-off.

Below: which states actually have licensed online poker, what regulation genuinely buys a player, where offshore rooms still hold real advantages, and how to decide which side of the line suits your game.

Which states have regulated online poker

Only a small number of US states have licensed real-money online poker, and the list has grown far more slowly than online casino and sports betting. Nevada and Delaware were first out of the gate over a decade ago, New Jersey followed, and Pennsylvania and Michigan came later with much larger populations behind them. West Virginia passed enabling legislation but has never produced meaningful poker traffic, and Connecticut and Rhode Island have licensed online gaming without a live poker product of any scale.

The defining constraint has never been the law itself — it has been liquidity. Online poker only works when there are enough players at the tables, and a single state, even a populous one, struggles to sustain a full schedule around the clock. That is why interstate compacts matter so much. The multi-state agreement that pools players across several licensed states is the only reason regulated US poker has a functioning tournament calendar at all, and its scope has been the subject of years of legal wrangling.

Everywhere else — which is most of the country, including enormous poker markets like Texas, Florida, California and New York — there is no licensed online poker option. Players in those states either drive to a card room or play offshore. We cover the two largest of those markets in more detail in our guide to offshore poker in Texas and Florida.

What regulation genuinely gives you

It is worth being specific rather than hand-waving about safety, because the real benefits are concrete and limited.

  • Segregated player funds. Licensed operators are required to hold player balances separately from operating capital, so a company in financial trouble cannot spend your bankroll.
  • A regulator to complain to. If a dispute goes badly, there is a state gaming commission with actual authority over the operator's licence. That is meaningful leverage that no offshore player has.
  • Audited game integrity. Random number generators and game software are tested by approved labs on a schedule.
  • Banking that works with US institutions. Regulated sites accept ordinary debit cards and bank transfers without the friction offshore players deal with routinely.
  • Clear tax reporting. Licensed operators issue the paperwork the IRS expects, which simplifies an area that trips up plenty of players.

None of that is trivial. If you are depositing serious money and you live in a licensed state, those protections have real value and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise.

Where offshore rooms still win

The offshore case is equally concrete, and it comes down to four things: access, traffic, bonuses and banking speed.

Access is the obvious one. If you live in one of the forty-plus states without licensed online poker, offshore rooms are the only online option that exists. That is not a preference; it is the entire market.

Traffic and schedule favour offshore more often than regulated advocates like to admit. Because offshore rooms draw from a continent-wide player pool rather than a state or a small compact, they can run large-guarantee tournament series, deep satellite ladders and around-the-clock cash games that a single-state operator simply cannot support. The flagship series at rooms like Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker carry guarantees well beyond anything a regulated US site runs.

Bonus value is the third gap, and it is a wide one. Regulated operators work under advertising rules, tax burdens and compliance costs that constrain how aggressive their promotions can be. Offshore rooms compete for players almost entirely on offer size, which is why the welcome packages on our best poker bonuses page are consistently larger than anything a state-licensed room advertises. Rakeback and loyalty programmes follow the same pattern.

Crypto banking is the fourth. Offshore rooms built their payment infrastructure around Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies out of necessity, and the result is withdrawal speed that regulated sites, tied to traditional banking rails, cannot match. Cashouts measured in hours rather than days are routine at rooms like BetOnline Poker and TigerGaming.

The risks nobody should gloss over

Offshore play carries genuine trade-offs, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

There is no US regulator standing behind your balance. If an offshore operator decides to slow-roll a withdrawal or close an account, your recourse is the operator's own support department and whatever reputational pressure the poker community can apply. That has generally worked out for the long-established rooms, but it is a fundamentally weaker position than a licensed player is in.

Game integrity is self-policed. Offshore rooms publish their own security measures and respond to community pressure when problems surface, but there is no state-approved testing lab signing off on the software. And the legal position for the player, while overwhelmingly focused on operators rather than individuals in US federal law, varies enough by state that it is worth reading the terms rather than assuming.

How the two compare on the numbers that matter

Strip away the rhetoric and the choice tends to come down to what you personally optimise for.

  1. If you value fund security and regulatory recourse above everything, and you live in a licensed state, play regulated. That is the correct answer for a lot of people.
  2. If you want the biggest tournament guarantees and the deepest satellite schedules, offshore rooms have a structural advantage that regulation is unlikely to close soon.
  3. If bonus value and rakeback drive your win rate, offshore is clearly ahead, and it is not close.
  4. If you need same-day withdrawals, crypto-native offshore banking beats regulated bank transfers comfortably.
  5. If you live outside a licensed state, the comparison is academic — offshore or a live card room are your only options.

What about Canada?

Canadian players face a related but distinct picture. Ontario built a regulated market with international operators participating, while the rest of the country operates under a patchwork of provincial arrangements and long-standing tolerance of offshore play. The result is that Ontario residents have a genuine regulated choice with the same bonus-value trade-off Americans see, while players elsewhere in Canada largely default to offshore rooms. Our Canada guide covers the provincial detail.

Where things go from here

The realistic outlook is slow movement. Online poker legislation has consistently trailed both sports betting and online casino, largely because poker generates far less tax revenue per player and therefore commands far less legislative attention. States looking at internet gaming bills tend to prioritise slots and table games, with poker attached as an afterthought if at all.

Interstate liquidity remains the variable that matters most. If more licensed states join the shared player pool, regulated poker becomes a genuinely competitive product rather than a thin one. Until then, the structural advantages offshore rooms hold on traffic, series scale and promotional generosity are likely to persist.

The bottom line

Regulated US online poker is safer in the ways that regulation is designed to make things safer, and thinner in almost every way that affects a player's day-to-day experience. Offshore rooms offer bigger fields, far bigger bonuses and faster crypto cashouts, at the cost of formal protection. Neither is universally correct — the right answer depends on where you live, how much you deposit and what you actually want from the game.
