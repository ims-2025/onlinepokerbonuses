Rakeback Wars: How WPN's 65% Elite Benefits Stacks

For regular players, rakeback is worth more than any welcome bonus over time. We break down the two dominant rakeback models on US- and Canada-facing

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published July 10, 2026·8 min read

There's a piece of advice that separates experienced online players from beginners: over the long run, rakeback is worth more than any welcome bonus. A welcome match is a one-time event; rakeback pays you back on every hand you play, indefinitely. If you play more than a few times a week, the rakeback program attached to your room may be the single most important factor in your bottom line — more important than the bonus that got you in the door.

The two networks that dominate the US and Canada market take very different approaches to rakeback, and the better fit depends entirely on how you play. Here's how the Winning Poker Network's Elite Benefits compares to the Chico Network's weekly cash model.

First, what is rakeback?

Every time you play a real-money hand, the room takes a small cut of the pot (in cash games) or a fee from the buy-in (in tournaments). That's the rake — it's how poker sites make money. Rakeback returns a percentage of that rake to you. If you generate $1,000 in rake over a month and your effective rakeback is 40%, you get $400 back. It's a rebate on the cost of playing, and it accrues whether you win or lose at the tables.

This is why rakeback compounds in importance with volume. A casual player who rakes $50 a month sees modest returns; a grinder raking thousands sees rakeback become a core revenue stream. If the concept is new, our overview of poker bonus types puts rakeback in context alongside welcome and reload offers.

WPN's Elite Benefits: up to 65%, tier-based

On the Winning Poker Network — home to Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker — rakeback comes through the Elite Benefits program. The headline number is generous: up to 65% of your rake returned. The catch, and it's a fair one, is that the top percentages are tier-based. You climb the tiers by generating rake, and the more you play, the higher your rakeback rate rises.

This structure is built for dedicated players. If you put in serious volume, Elite Benefits rewards you with one of the highest rakeback ceilings available anywhere in the offshore market. If you play only occasionally, you'll sit in the lower tiers and see a more modest return. It's a program that scales with commitment — excellent for grinders, less impactful for the casual crowd.

Chico's weekly cash: flat, guaranteed, simple

The Chico Network — powering BetOnline, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag — takes the opposite philosophy. Rather than a tiered climb, Chico's model centers on guaranteed weekly rakeback paid as real cash. BetOnline's relaunched Poker Loyalty & Rewards program pays players a rebate every Friday, landing directly in the poker wallet with no tiers to unlock and no fine print to decode.

The appeal here is transparency and predictability. You always know roughly what you're getting back, it arrives on a set schedule, and it's real withdrawable cash rather than bonus credit that has to be cleared. For recreational and mid-stakes players who value simplicity, Chico's flat weekly model is often more satisfying than chasing a tier ladder — even if the ceiling is lower than WPN's 65%.

Head to head: who wins?

The honest answer is that it depends on your volume. High-volume grinders who can climb into WPN's upper tiers will extract more total value from Elite Benefits — 65% is simply a higher ceiling than the flat weekly model reaches. If you're playing enough to hit those tiers, WPN's rakeback is the stronger long-term proposition.

For everyone else — the recreational player, the mid-stakes regular, the person who plays a few nights a week — Chico's guaranteed weekly cash frequently delivers a better real-world experience. It's predictable, it's paid promptly, and it doesn't require you to grind toward a tier you may never reach. Value you actually receive beats value you theoretically qualify for.

The bonus-plus-rakeback play

The smartest approach combines both forms of value. When you first join a room, you're clearing your welcome bonus and accumulating rakeback simultaneously — the same hands work double duty. That's why the opening weeks on a new account are the most valuable period of your poker year, and why timing your deposit to coincide with heavy play makes such a difference. Our guide to claiming a poker bonus explains how to set that up correctly.

Once the welcome bonus is cleared, rakeback becomes your steady-state edge. That's the point at which the network you chose really starts to matter, because you'll be earning that rebate on every session for as long as you play there.

The bottom line

Rakeback is the quiet engine of long-term poker value, and the two networks serve different drivers. WPN's Elite Benefits rewards volume with a market-leading 65% ceiling; Chico's weekly cash rewards everyone with simple, guaranteed payouts. Match the model to your play style and you'll keep far more of what you generate at the tables.

Frequently asked questions

Is rakeback better than a welcome bonus?

For regular players, usually yes. A welcome bonus pays once; rakeback pays on every hand you play, indefinitely. The more volume you put in, the more rakeback outweighs a one-time bonus.

Do I have to opt in to rakeback?

It varies by room. Chico's weekly rakeback is typically automatic, while WPN's Elite Benefits accrues as you play and rises through tiers. Check each room's loyalty program details for specifics.
