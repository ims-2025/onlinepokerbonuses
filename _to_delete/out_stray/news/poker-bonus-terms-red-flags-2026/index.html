Poker Bonus Terms: 7 Red Flags Worth Reading

Most poker bonuses are honest but badly explained. Here are the seven clauses in the terms that decide whether an offer is worth your deposit.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published September 4, 2026·8 min read

Nobody reads the terms. That is not a moral failing, it is a design outcome — bonus terms are written by compliance departments to be complete rather than clear, and the interesting clauses are buried between paragraphs of boilerplate about account verification and jurisdictional restrictions. The result is that a large number of players deposit against an offer they have fundamentally misunderstood, clear a third of it, and conclude that poker bonuses are a scam.

They are mostly not a scam. The five rooms we cover run offers that pay out exactly as written. The problem is that what is written and what the player assumed are different things, and the gap is entirely visible in advance to anyone who knows which seven clauses to look for. This guide is those seven clauses. Read them against any offer on our best poker bonuses page and you will know within two minutes whether it suits how you actually play.

1. The release rate — the number that matters most

Every incremental poker bonus releases in chunks as you generate rake. The headline percentage tells you the ceiling; the release rate tells you whether you will ever reach it. A 100% up to $2,000 offer and a 100% up to $1,000 offer are not comparable until you know how much rake each one requires per dollar released.

The clause you are looking for typically reads something like "$1 of bonus is released for every $X in rake and tournament fees contributed." That X is the whole offer. A generous ceiling attached to a punishing release rate is worth less to a recreational player than a modest ceiling that pays out quickly, because the recreational player will never generate enough rake to touch the ceiling either way.

The practical test: estimate your monthly rake honestly, multiply by the release rate, and see how much of the bonus you would realistically capture in the time allowed. Most players discover they should be optimising for release speed rather than headline size — a point we develop at length in the bonus types guide.

2. The expiry window

Bonus funds expire. This is normal, it is disclosed, and it is the single most common reason players lose value they had already earned. Typical windows run from 30 to 90 days from deposit, and when the window closes, whatever remains unreleased is forfeited — not paused, not extended, forfeited.

Two details inside this clause routinely catch people out. First, the clock usually starts at deposit, not at first hand played, so a bonus claimed before a two-week holiday is already a quarter spent. Second, some rooms expire the whole bonus at once while others expire it in tranches, which changes whether a slow month costs you everything or only the last portion. Check which model applies before you deposit rather than after.

3. Which games and stakes actually count

Not all rake is created equal in the eyes of a bonus. Look for the qualifying-play clause and check the following:

  • Cash game rake versus tournament fees. Most offers count both, but the contribution rates sometimes differ, and a few exclude certain formats entirely.
  • Micro-stakes exclusions or reduced weighting. Some rooms weight the lowest limits at a fraction of full contribution, which quietly doubles the effective release rate for anyone playing there.
  • Fast-fold and lottery-format sit-and-gos. These usually count, and because they generate rake at a much higher hourly rate, they are the efficient way to clear — but confirm rather than assume.
  • Casino and sportsbook play on combined accounts. On rooms with a shared wallet, casino wagering typically does not contribute to a poker bonus, and in some cases touching it can complicate the bonus entirely.

This clause is also where the difference between the WPN and Chico rooms shows up most clearly, since network rake structures differ. The room-by-room detail is in our Black Chip Poker review and TigerGaming review.

4. The one-bonus-at-a-time rule

Nearly every room states that a player may hold only one active bonus at a time, and that claiming a new one may forfeit the unreleased balance of the old one. This sounds trivial until a reload offer lands in your inbox halfway through clearing a welcome bonus and you accept it reflexively.

The right habit is to check your unreleased balance before accepting anything, and to treat promotional emails as offers to be timed rather than buttons to be pressed. If the reload is genuinely better, take it deliberately, knowing what you are giving up. If it is not, it will usually come round again.

5. Bonus funds versus withdrawable cash

Read carefully whether the bonus, once released, is real cash in your balance or a restricted amount subject to further conditions. On the incremental rake-based model the five rooms we cover use, released bonus money is normally straightforward withdrawable cash, which is one of the reasons poker bonuses are structurally more honest than casino bonuses.

But the clause is worth checking anyway, particularly on combined poker-and-casino accounts where a single deposit can be attached to a casino-style bonus with genuine wagering requirements. A wagering requirement — the kind that says you must turn the funds over a multiple of times before withdrawal — is a completely different animal from a rake-based release, and confusing the two is how players end up trapped in an account they cannot cash out of.

6. Deposit method restrictions

A short clause with an outsized effect: some offers exclude certain deposit methods, and some crypto-specific bonuses are only available on crypto deposits. If you deposit by card and then discover the enhanced offer required Bitcoin, the room is under no obligation to retroactively apply it — and support typically cannot fix it even when they would like to.

The related trap is the promo code. Codes generally must be entered at the moment of deposit, and a deposit made without one is usually final. Take the extra thirty seconds to confirm the code is live and correctly entered; our how to claim walkthrough covers the exact sequence for each room.

7. The bonus-abuse and account-integrity language

Every set of terms contains a section reserving the room's right to void bonuses for abuse. This is not there to trap honest players — it exists because bonus abuse is real and the rooms lose money to it. But it is worth knowing what triggers it, because a few behaviours that seem innocent are on the list:

  1. Multiple accounts from one household or IP. Rooms detect this reliably and the consequences extend beyond the bonus.
  2. Depositing and immediately withdrawing without meaningful play, repeatedly.
  3. Chip dumping or collusive play, including well-intentioned transfers between friends.
  4. Name and payment-detail mismatches, which usually surface at withdrawal rather than deposit and can stall a cashout for weeks.

The fourth is the one that catches ordinary players. Deposit under a name or wallet that does not match your verified identity and the money will go in fine and come out slowly, if at all, until documents reconcile. Verify the account early, while nothing is at stake.

How to read an offer in two minutes

Put the seven together and the assessment is quick. Find the release rate, find the expiry window, and multiply your realistic monthly rake by the days available. That gives you the amount of bonus you will actually collect, which is the only number worth comparing between offers. Then confirm your preferred games qualify, your deposit method is eligible, and you are not sacrificing a partially cleared bonus to claim it.

Do that and the whole category stops being a gamble. The bonuses on offer at the rooms serving US and Canadian players are real money — often several hundred dollars a year for a modest-volume player — and the only thing standing between you and it is two minutes of reading that almost nobody does. Compare current offers on our best poker bonuses page, check the eligibility notes for your region on the Canada page if that is where you play, and claim deliberately rather than reflexively.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important clause in a poker bonus?

The release rate — how much rake you must generate per dollar of bonus released. Combined with the expiry window, it determines how much of the headline figure you will actually collect.

Do poker bonuses have wagering requirements?

Not usually. Poker bonuses release incrementally as you generate rake, and released funds are normally withdrawable cash. Casino-style wagering requirements can apply on combined poker-and-casino accounts, so check which bonus you are claiming.
