WPN vs Chico: Which Poker Network Offers Better Value

The two networks that power every major US- and Canada-facing poker room take very different approaches to bonuses, tournaments and rakeback.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published July 12, 2026·8 min read

If you play online poker from the US or Canada, there's a good chance you're playing on one of two networks whether you realize it or not. The Winning Poker Network (WPN) and the Chico Poker Network sit behind nearly every major offshore room, and the brand you choose largely determines the player pool you face, the tournaments you can enter and the bonus you clear. Knowing how the two stack up is one of the most useful things a serious player can learn.

This isn't a case of one network being objectively better than the other. They're built for slightly different players. The right choice depends on whether you prioritize tournament guarantees, cash-game softness, bonus size or rakeback — so let's break it down category by category.

The rooms on each network

On the Winning Poker Network, the flagship is Americas Cardroom, with Black Chip Poker running as a sister skin on the same player pool. Both share the same tournaments, the same cash games and the same Elite Benefits rakeback program — the main difference between them is the welcome offer's minimum deposit and a handful of brand-specific promotions.

The Chico Network is anchored by BetOnline, with TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag sharing its tables. As with WPN's skins, players on all three Chico rooms are seated in the same games, so your choice among them comes down to promotions, interface preference and which sportsbook or casino you want bundled into the same wallet.

Bonuses: WPN goes bigger

On headline welcome-bonus size, WPN has the edge. Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker both offer a 100% match up to $2,000, the largest first-deposit bonuses available to US and Canada players. The Chico rooms counter with a 100% match up to $1,000 — a smaller ceiling, but one that's often easier for a recreational player to fully clear given the lower total rake requirement.

That distinction matters more than the raw number suggests. A bonus you can only clear a fraction of is worth less than a smaller bonus you clear in full. High-volume grinders will extract more from WPN's $2,000 ceiling; casual players may find the Chico $1,000 offer a better fit. If the mechanics of clearing are unfamiliar, our explainer on poker bonus types covers how these offers release in detail.

Tournaments: WPN's biggest strength

This is where the Winning Poker Network pulls clearly ahead. WPN's tournament schedule is the deepest in the offshore market, headlined by the multi-million-dollar Venom and supported by the daily OSS Cub3d series and the mid-stakes MOSH schedule. If your game is multi-table tournaments and you want the biggest guarantees available to US players, WPN is the obvious home.

Chico's tournament offering is respectable — daily guarantees, a busy Sunday program and frequent step satellites — but it's aimed more at recreational and mid-stakes players than at those chasing seven-figure prize pools. For the average tournament player this is perfectly adequate; for the ambitious grinder, WPN's guarantees are hard to walk away from.

Cash games: Chico's soft edge

Flip to cash games and the picture changes. Chico's tables are widely regarded as some of the softest online, thanks to a steady flow of recreational players crossing over from BetOnline's sportsbook and casino. For a winning cash-game player, the quality of the opposition matters more than any bonus, and Chico's recreational-heavy pool is a genuine draw.

WPN's cash games are busier in terms of raw traffic, particularly during Venom season, but they also attract a higher proportion of regulars and grinders. Both networks offer fast-fold and jackpot Sit & Go formats for players who want quicker action. The rule of thumb: if you grind cash and want soft games, lean Chico; if you want the deepest traffic and don't mind tougher spots, WPN delivers volume.

Rakeback: two philosophies

Rakeback is where the networks diverge philosophically. WPN's Elite Benefits program can return up to 65% of your rake, but it's tier-based — you unlock higher percentages by putting in volume, which rewards dedicated players. Chico's approach is flatter and more transparent: guaranteed weekly rakeback paid as real cash, most notably BetOnline's Friday payout that lands directly in your poker wallet.

Neither is universally better. High-volume players who climb the tiers will squeeze more from WPN's 65% ceiling. Recreational and mid-stakes players often prefer Chico's simplicity — a predictable weekly rebate with no tiers to chase. We took a closer look at that structure in our coverage of BetOnline's weekly rakeback, and it remains one of the clearest value propositions in the offshore market.

The verdict

For tournament players and high-volume grinders who want the biggest bonus and the deepest guarantees, the Winning Poker Network is the stronger choice. For cash-game players who prize soft tables and a simple, guaranteed weekly rebate, Chico is hard to beat. Many experienced players simply keep an account on both and move between them depending on the week's promotions and where the games are best.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have accounts on both WPN and Chico?

Yes. Many players keep an account on at least one room from each network so they can move to wherever the games are softest and the promotions are best on any given week. Each account is eligible for its own welcome bonus.

Which network has softer games?

Chico's cash games are generally considered softer thanks to recreational crossover from its sportsbook and casino, while WPN offers deeper overall traffic and far bigger tournament guarantees.
