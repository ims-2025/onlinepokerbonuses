Poker HUDs and Tracking Software: The 2026 Rules

Most US-facing poker rooms now ban HUDs outright. Here is what tracking software is still allowed, what is banned, and how to study without one.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 22, 2026·9 min read

For most of online poker's first two decades, a heads-up display was simply part of the furniture. You opened your tracker, you opened your tables, and a small block of statistics floated over every opponent telling you how often they raised, folded and three-bet. Serious players considered it non-negotiable. Recreational players mostly did not know it existed — which was rather the point.

That era is over on the sites most American and Canadian players actually use. The rooms serving this market have spent years moving away from third-party tracking, and the practical position in 2026 is that a live HUD is either forbidden or functionally useless at nearly every table you can sit at. If you are choosing where to play, or coming back after a break, this is one of the bigger structural differences between today's games and the ones you remember. Why the rooms turned against HUDs

The argument against tracking software is not that it cheats. A HUD only shows information that was, in principle, available to anyone watching every hand — it just remembers it perfectly and displays it instantly. The argument is about asymmetry.

A regular playing forty hours a week with a database of millions of hands has an enormous informational edge over someone who plays on a Thursday evening for fun. Not because the regular is better at poker, though they usually are, but because the software converts hours of prior grinding into an in-the-moment advantage the recreational player cannot see and cannot match. Over time that accelerates the transfer of money from casual players to professionals, and the casual players quietly stop depositing.

Every operator eventually reaches the same conclusion: the recreational player is the ecosystem. A poker room that becomes uncomfortable for beginners does not stay a poker room for very long. Banning or neutralising HUDs is the single most direct lever available, which is why so many rooms have pulled it.

The anonymity approach

The most effective anti-HUD measure is not a rule at all — it is anonymous tables. If every seat shows a generic label instead of a persistent screen name, there is nothing for a tracker to attach a history to. Your database can record the hands, but it cannot tell you that the player in seat four is the same person you played four hundred hands against last Tuesday.

Anonymity has spread across the US-facing market in various forms. Some rooms apply it to cash games while leaving tournaments identified, since tournament fields are transient anyway. Some rotate aliases per session. Some let players change their screen name periodically, which achieves a softer version of the same thing.

The effect on play is significant and largely positive for casual players. Without persistent identities, nobody can build a profile on you, nobody can seat-select against you specifically, and the bum-hunting behaviour that plagued identified games — strong players hunting weak ones and refusing to play each other — becomes much harder to organise.

What is still allowed

The blanket statement 'HUDs are banned' is close enough to true for live play, but the tooling picture is more nuanced than that, and the distinctions matter.

  • Real-time HUDs overlaid on tables. Prohibited at the rooms most US players use, and largely pointless where anonymity applies. This is the category that has genuinely gone away.
  • Post-session hand history review. Generally fine and genuinely valuable. Reviewing your own play after the fact is study, not an in-game edge, and it is how most improvement actually happens.
  • Solvers and equity calculators used away from the table. Standard practice for studying. Using one *during* a hand is a different matter and is prohibited everywhere.
  • Seating scripts and auto-seaters. Banned essentially universally. These automate table selection to grab seats against weak players and are treated as harshly as bots.
  • Data-mining and hand-history sharing services. Banned. Buying databases of hands you were not dealt into is a serious terms violation at every major room.
  • Built-in room statistics. Some clients offer basic session or hand statistics natively. Anything the operator provides is allowed by definition.

The important line is between studying away from the table and receiving assistance at the table. Almost every room draws it in roughly the same place, but the exact wording differs, and the penalties for getting it wrong — frozen balances, confiscated funds, closed accounts — are severe enough to make reading the actual terms worthwhile before you install anything.

How this changes the way you should play

If you learned online poker in the tracking era, the adjustment is real. You lose three things: opponent profiling, seat selection based on history, and the safety net of glancing at a number instead of forming a read. What you get back is a game where those advantages are gone for everyone, including the players who used to have far better versions of them than you did.

Practically, the skills that matter go up in value:

  1. Live reads on betting patterns. Sizing tells, timing patterns and bet-frequency observations made in real time — the things a HUD used to do for you, done by paying attention.
  2. Note-taking. Where identities persist, manual notes are usually permitted and are now the main way to carry information forward. A short, specific note beats a long vague one.
  3. Solid default strategy. With less opponent-specific information available, a well-constructed baseline game does more of the work. This is where off-table study pays off.
  4. Table awareness. Stack sizes, position, who has been active in the last orbit. Cheap information that most players ignore.
  5. Discipline about session length. Without a HUD flagging the weak seats, marginal games are harder to identify, so leaving a bad table promptly matters more.

There is an underrated benefit here for anyone rebuilding a bankroll. Games without tracking software are meaningfully softer for competent-but-not-professional players, because the semi-pro grinder who relied on statistical crutches has lost more than you have. Comparing the networks

The two networks serving most of this market have taken broadly similar directions with different emphases. The Winning Poker Network rooms — including Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker — have leaned into anonymity and anti-tool enforcement as part of a long-running game-integrity programme. The Chico network rooms, including BetOnline Poker and its sister sites, have taken a comparable line with their own implementation details.

What this means when you are choosing a room is that HUD policy is no longer a meaningful differentiator between the major US-facing options. You will not find a mainstream room that lets you run a full tracker against identified opponents. Choose on the things that still vary: payout reliability, traffic at the stakes and formats you play, tournament schedule quality, banking options and the actual clearing terms attached to the welcome bonus.

Rules of thumb worth following

  • Read the terms before you install any third-party tool. 'I did not know' has never recovered a confiscated balance.
  • Never run anything that acts on your behalf. Auto-seating, auto-folding, hotkey automation and bots are the fastest route to a closed account.
  • Keep your study off-table. Solvers, ranges and hand review between sessions are fine and are where improvement comes from anyway.
  • Take notes where you can. They are the legitimate replacement for the profiling a HUD used to do.
  • Do not buy hand histories. Data-mining services are banned everywhere and buying from one puts your account at risk.
  • Assume table anonymity is the default. Build a strategy that does not depend on knowing who you are playing.

The bottom line

The HUD era ended because it had to. Games that let a minority of players carry a permanent informational advantage against everyone else do not stay populated, and operators worked that out. The result is a market where third-party tracking is off the table and the games are, on balance, healthier for it.

If you are returning to online poker after time away, the practical takeaway is that your old software will not help you and your fundamentals will matter more than they used to. Frequently asked questions

Are poker HUDs banned at US-facing poker sites?

At the major rooms serving US and Canadian players, yes — real-time heads-up displays are prohibited, and widespread table anonymity makes them largely ineffective even where enforcement is imperfect.

Can I still review my own hand histories after a session?

Generally yes. Reviewing your own play away from the table is study rather than in-game assistance, and it is permitted at most rooms. Buying or sharing mined hand histories is not.
