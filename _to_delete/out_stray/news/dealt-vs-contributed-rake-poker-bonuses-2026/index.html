Dealt vs Contributed Rake: How Poker Bonuses Count It

The rake method a poker room uses decides how fast your bonus clears. Learn dealt vs contributed rake and how to pick offers that suit your playing style.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published September 27, 2026·7 min read

Every poker bonus and rakeback program is ultimately paid out of rake. The welcome offer promises a percentage match, the loyalty scheme promises points, and the rakeback deal promises a share — but none of those numbers mean anything until you know how the room decides how much rake you paid. That single accounting choice can change how fast a bonus clears by a wide margin, and it rewards some playing styles far more than others.

There are two broad methods, called dealt and contributed rake, plus a hybrid that sits between them. This guide explains each one in plain terms, walks through a worked example, and shows how to use the method a room uses to pick the offer that actually fits your game. What rake is and why the method matters

Rake is the small percentage the room takes from each pot in cash games, up to a fixed cap. In tournaments the equivalent is the entry fee, and that part is simple: the fee is yours alone, so tournament players always get credit for exactly what they paid. Cash games are different, because a single rake amount is taken from a pot built by several players, and the room has to decide how to split credit for it.

That credit is what drives almost everything that matters to a bonus hunter: how many loyalty points you earn, how quickly a deposit bonus releases, where you place on rake races and leaderboards, and how much rakeback you receive. Two players at the same table in the same hands can be credited very different amounts depending on the method.

Dealt rake: everyone at the table shares equally

Under the dealt method, every player who was dealt cards in a raked hand receives an equal share of the rake, regardless of whether they put a single chip in the pot. If a six-handed pot is raked $3, each of the six players is credited with $0.50.

  • Who it favours: tight and passive players, multi-tablers who fold a lot, and short-session recreational players who see many hands but play few pots.
  • Who it hurts: aggressive regulars who play lots of pots, since they drive the rake but share the credit with players who folded preflop.
  • Main quirk: you can earn rake credit in a hand you folded immediately, as long as the pot reached the flop and was raked.

Many rooms apply a no-flop, no-drop rule, which means hands that end preflop are not raked at all. Under the dealt method, that means credit only arrives from hands that saw a flop — but you still receive it even if you were no longer in the hand.

Contributed rake: credit follows your chips

Under the contributed method, rake credit is split in proportion to how much each player put into the pot. If you contributed half the money in a raked pot, you are credited with half the rake. Players who folded without investing receive nothing.

  • Who it favours: loose-aggressive players and anyone who plays a lot of pots, especially big ones.
  • Who it hurts: tight players and heavy multi-tablers who fold most hands, because their credit per hour drops sharply.
  • Main quirk: your earnings are much more variable from session to session, since they track the size of the pots you played rather than the number of hands you saw.

Weighted contributed: the common middle ground

Many modern rooms use a weighted contributed method, which splits rake by contribution but caps each player's counted contribution at the amount the second-biggest contributor put in. That stops one player from being credited for an oversized shove that nobody called, and it smooths out some of the extremes of pure contributed rake.

In practice, weighted contributed behaves like contributed rake for most hands, with the edges filed off. It still rewards players who put money in the pot rather than players who simply sit at the table.

A worked example

Imagine a six-handed pot of $60 raked $3. Player A put in $30, Player B put in $20, Player C put in $10, and the other three players folded preflop without posting a blind.

  • Dealt: all six players are credited $0.50 each — including the three who folded.
  • Contributed: A is credited $1.50, B $1.00, C $0.50, and the three folders receive nothing.
  • Weighted contributed: A's counted contribution is capped at B's $20, so the $3 is split 20:20:10 — A and B receive $1.20 each, C receives $0.60.

Scale that across thousands of hands and the gap is meaningful. A tight player at a dealt-rake room may clear a bonus noticeably faster than the same player at a contributed-rake room, while a loose-aggressive player might see the opposite.

How to use this when choosing a bonus

Rooms usually disclose their method in the loyalty or VIP terms, sometimes under a heading like "how points are calculated". The details change, so always check the current terms before you commit a large deposit. Then match the method to your style:

  1. Estimate your VPIP. If you play fewer than about one in five hands, dealt rake is likely to credit you more. If you play loose and see lots of flops, contributed methods tend to work better.
  2. Factor in table count. Multi-tabling many tight tables at once magnifies the advantage of the dealt method.
  3. Look beyond cash games. If you mostly play tournaments or sit-and-gos, the method matters much less, because entry fees are credited directly to you.
  4. Compare the clearing rate, not just the match. A generous match with a harsh clearing rate under a method that disfavours your style can be worth less than a smaller, friendlier offer.

Common misunderstandings

  • "Dealt rake is always better." Only for tight or passive players. Aggressive players can earn noticeably more under contributed methods.
  • "My points are wrong because I folded." Under a contributed method, folding preflop earns nothing — that is by design, not an error.
  • "Rakeback percentage is all that matters." A higher rakeback percentage on a method that credits you less can pay out less than a lower percentage on a method that suits you.
  • "Tournament players need to worry about this." Entry fees are credited in full to the player who paid them, so the method is essentially a cash-game issue.

The bottom line

Dealt rake rewards being at the table; contributed rake rewards being in the pot. Neither is objectively better — the right one is whichever matches how you actually play. Knowing the difference lets you read a bonus offer the way the room does and pick the deal that clears fastest for you, not for an imaginary average player.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between dealt and contributed rake?

Dealt rake splits credit equally among everyone dealt into a raked hand. Contributed rake splits it in proportion to how much money each player put into the pot.

Which rake method clears poker bonuses faster?

Tight players who fold often usually clear faster under dealt rake, while loose-aggressive players who play many pots tend to earn more under contributed methods.
