PLO vs Hold'em: Which Clears Poker Bonuses Faster?

Pot-Limit Omaha generates rake far faster than Hold'em, which speeds up bonus clearing. Here is the trade-off every bonus hunter should understand first.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 18, 2026·9 min read

Every deposit bonus at the rooms we cover is released in increments as you generate rake. That single mechanic means the game you choose to play is not just a matter of taste — it directly determines how quickly the pending balance sitting in your account turns into withdrawable cash. And there is one game that clears bonuses dramatically faster than No-Limit Hold'em: Pot-Limit Omaha.

That fact gets repeated constantly in bonus-hunting circles, usually without the caveat that matters. Yes, PLO clears faster. It also has far higher variance and a steeper learning curve, and a player who switches games purely to speed up a bonus can lose more at the tables than the bonus was ever worth. This article walks through why the rake difference exists, roughly how large it is, and when the trade actually makes sense. If you are still choosing where to deposit, start with our comparison of the current best poker bonuses and come back to the game-selection question afterwards.

Why bonus clearing is really a rake question

Welcome offers at Winning Poker Network and Chico rooms are almost universally structured as incremental release deals. You deposit, the bonus sits in a pending pot, and the room drips it into your cash balance as you contribute rake — typically in fixed chunks tied to loyalty points or a straight rake-contributed figure.

The consequence is that clearing speed has nothing to do with whether you win or lose. A losing player who plays high-rake games clears faster than a winning player who plays low-rake ones. The room does not care about your results; it cares about the rake your seat generates. Our bonus types explainer covers the different release structures in detail, but the short version is that for a standard 100% match, the question 'how fast will this clear?' is identical to 'how much rake will I generate per hour?'

The structural reason PLO rakes more

In cash games, rake is taken as a percentage of the pot up to a capped maximum. The cap is the crucial part. In a typical low-stakes game, the cap is reached in most pots that go past the flop, meaning the room takes the full maximum rather than a percentage.

Now consider how the two games actually play. In No-Limit Hold'em, a large share of hands end pre-flop or on the flop with a single bet taking it down. Those pots are small, often below the rake cap, and generate a fraction of the maximum. Pot-Limit Omaha is the opposite. Every player holds four cards, equities run much closer together, and far more hands go multi-way to a flop and beyond. Pots are bigger and they get bigger earlier.

The result is that a much higher proportion of PLO pots hit the rake cap. Combine that with a higher hands-per-hour rate at full-ring PLO tables — fewer walkovers, more action — and the rake generated per hour of play at the same nominal stakes is meaningfully higher in Omaha than in Hold'em. Estimates vary by room, stake and table size, so treat any specific multiplier you see quoted with suspicion, but the direction of the effect is not in dispute.

The trade-off nobody mentions in the forums

Here is where the simple advice falls apart. Rake is a cost you pay to the room. Generating more rake per hour means losing more money to the house per hour, and the bonus only partially rebates that. A typical incremental-release welcome offer returns something well under half of the rake you generate while clearing it.

So the honest framing is: PLO gets you to the finish line faster, but the race itself is more expensive. That is fine if you play PLO competently, because the games are generally softer than Hold'em at equivalent stakes and a competent player's edge can exceed the extra rake drag. It is a disaster if you are a Hold'em regular dabbling in Omaha for the first time, because you will be paying premium rake to play a game you do not yet understand, against opponents who do.

  • If you already play PLO: the faster clear is close to free value. Play your normal game and let the bonus release quicker.
  • If you play only Hold'em: the bonus you clear faster is very likely smaller than the extra you lose learning Omaha at real stakes.
  • If you want to learn PLO anyway: drop two or three stake levels below your Hold'em game while you learn. The clearing speed advantage largely survives the move down; your bankroll survives too.
  • If you are clearing on a deadline: check the expiry terms first. Some offers have generous windows that make the game switch unnecessary.

Where PLO actually runs at the covered rooms

Liquidity matters more than theory. A game that clears bonuses twice as fast is worth nothing if there is no table running when you sit down.

Across the five rooms we cover, PLO traffic is real but concentrated. The Winning Poker Network rooms — Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker — share a pool and generally hold the strongest Omaha cash-game numbers of the operators on our list, with the deepest action clustered at low-to-mid stakes during US evening hours. On the Chico side, BetOnline Poker, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag also share a pool, so a PLO table you see at one is the same table you see at the others.

Practical advice: open the lobby at the hours you actually intend to play, not at the moment you are reading about it, and count the running tables. Peak liquidity for both networks skews heavily toward US evenings, and off-peak PLO can be thin enough that you end up short-handed or waiting. Players in Canada on Pacific time often find the best overlap in the mid-to-late evening.

PLO tournaments and the clearing question

Tournaments complicate the picture. Tournament rake is the entry fee, a fixed and transparent amount taken once at registration rather than pot by pot. That means a PLO tournament and a Hold'em tournament at the same buy-in generate essentially the same rake, and the clearing-speed argument disappears entirely.

What tournaments do offer is predictability. You know exactly what rake a session will generate before you register, which makes planning a clearing schedule far easier than estimating cash-game hands per hour. Some bonus hunters use a hybrid approach: cash-game PLO for raw clearing volume, plus a fixed tournament allocation for the upside and the series value.

A sane clearing plan

Whatever game you land on, the mechanics of a good plan are the same. Work out the total rake required to release the full bonus, divide by a realistic estimate of your hourly rake generation, and check the result against the offer's expiry window. If the arithmetic says you need forty hours a week to clear in time, the honest answer is to claim a smaller bonus you can actually finish.

  1. Read the offer terms and find the exact rake-to-release ratio before depositing.
  2. Estimate your realistic weekly volume in hours, not in optimistic hands.
  3. Pick the game you play best at the stake your bankroll supports, then adjust for clearing speed — in that order, never the reverse.
  4. Track your progress weekly rather than assuming it will work out.
  5. If you fall behind, drop stakes and add tables rather than jumping to a game you do not know.

The bottom line

PLO clears poker bonuses faster because it produces bigger pots more often, and bigger pots hit the rake cap. That advantage is real and it is worth using — but only by players who can hold their own in the game. Switching to Omaha purely to speed up a bonus is a classic case of optimising the wrong number: the bonus is a rebate on rake, not a prize, and paying more rake to collect a rebate faster is only a win if your win rate covers the difference.

Frequently asked questions

Does PLO really clear poker bonuses faster than Hold'em?

Generally yes. Omaha pots go multi-way more often and hit the rake cap more frequently, so the same stake generates more rake per hour and releases incremental bonuses quicker.

Should I switch to PLO just to clear a welcome bonus?

Only if you can play it competently. Higher rake means higher cost per hour, and a Hold'em regular learning Omaha at real stakes usually loses more than the faster clear is worth.
