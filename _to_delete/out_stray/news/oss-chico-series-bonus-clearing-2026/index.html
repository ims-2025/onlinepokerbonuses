The OSS Advantage: Clearing Your Chico Bonus During

The Online Super Series turns the Chico Network into a rake-generating machine. Here's how US and Canada players can pair the OSS schedule with a welcome

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 9, 2026·8 min read

When bonus hunters on the Winning Poker Network talk about their calendar, the Venom dominates the conversation. But players on the other major offshore network have their own headline event, and it deserves just as much attention from anyone thinking about where to deposit. The Online Super Series — the OSS — is the Chico Network's signature multi-week tournament festival, and it creates one of the best windows of the year to fund an account, clear a welcome bonus and stack rakeback all at once. If you're weighing up your options on our best poker bonuses page, understanding how the OSS works can meaningfully change how you time your deposit.

The logic is the same one that makes any big tournament series valuable to bonus clearers: huge guarantees attract huge fields, and huge fields flood the schedule with satellites, freerolls and step tickets for weeks on end. For a player who wants to turn a 100% deposit match into real, released cash, a series like the OSS is a gift — a compressed period packed with qualifying action across every buy-in level.

What the Online Super Series actually is

The OSS is a recurring tournament festival run across the Chico Network's poker rooms — BetOnline Poker, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag. Because all three rooms share the same underlying player pool, an OSS event carries the combined liquidity of the entire network. That shared traffic is the whole point: it lets a mid-tier network put up guarantees big enough to compete for attention with the largest offshore sites, and it means the satellite schedule feeding those events is deep and constant.

A typical series runs for one to two weeks and spans dozens of numbered events across formats — regular hold'em, pot-limit Omaha, bounty tournaments, turbos and a marquee Main Event with the largest guarantee of the run. Buy-ins scale from low-stakes satellites that cost a few dollars all the way up to high-roller flights, so there's a lane for every bankroll. That range is exactly what makes it useful for bonus clearing, because you can choose the stakes that match the volume you actually want to put in.

Why a tournament series is built for bonus clearing

Most welcome bonuses on offshore rooms don't drop into your account as instant cash. They release incrementally as you generate rake — a rebate you earn back through play rather than a gift handed over up front. If that mechanic is new to you, our breakdown of how poker bonuses work walks through the clearing process in detail. The key takeaway is that clearing speed is a function of volume: the more raked hands and tournament fees you generate, the faster your pending bonus converts to withdrawable cash.

This is precisely where a series like the OSS shines. Tournament buy-ins generate rake efficiently, and a festival schedule gives you a dense, back-to-back run of events to register for. Instead of grinding scattered cash-game sessions over a couple of months hoping to clear before the bonus expires, you can front-load your volume into a two-week window where the games are running around the clock and the fields are soft with recreational players chasing a series score.

  • Satellites let you play into big Main Events for a fraction of the buy-in while still generating rake.
  • Bounty and mystery-bounty events add an extra equity stream on top of the standard prize pool.
  • Turbo and hyper formats let high-volume players rack up buy-ins — and clearing points — quickly.
  • Low-stakes flights keep the whole thing accessible for players clearing a modest first deposit.

The rakeback layer most players ignore

There's a second stream of value running underneath the bonus, and casual players routinely leave it on the table: rakeback. While you're clearing your welcome bonus during the OSS, you're simultaneously accumulating loyalty value through the Chico Network's rewards program. Every tournament fee and every raked hand counts toward that program, so a busy series week can push you into a better rakeback tier and pay you back a meaningful slice of what you contributed. Our deep dive on Chico Network value covers how the three rooms compare on this front.

Stack the two together and the math gets attractive. During an OSS run, three things are working at once: the equity you're playing for in the tournaments themselves, the welcome-bonus dollars releasing as you rake, and the rakeback landing in your account afterward. None of those is a promise of profit — poker is still poker, and variance in tournaments is brutal — but together they lower your effective cost of entry and improve your long-run expectation.

Timing your deposit around the series

The strategic question is when to fund your account. Deposit too early and your bonus clock may tick down before the biggest satellites arrive; deposit too late and you'll miss the ramp-up. Chico welcome bonuses typically give you a fixed window to clear, so the sweet spot is usually to deposit as the satellite schedule begins filling out, then concentrate your volume into the days with the most qualifying action. Check the series schedule in advance, identify the events you actually want to play, and work backward from there.

If you're brand new to the room, don't rush the deposit itself. Spend a few minutes with our walkthrough on how to claim a poker bonus so you enter the correct code in the cashier and don't accidentally forfeit the offer — the single most common and most expensive mistake new players make. A mistyped or missing code can cost you the entire bonus, and no series schedule can make up for that.

OSS value for cash-game players too

You don't have to be a tournament specialist to benefit from series season. The influx of recreational players chasing an OSS score softens the cash games considerably. Tables that normally play tight loosen up as satellite winners and hopeful qualifiers sit down between events, which is good news for winning cash players who prefer ring games to multi-table marathons. If cash is your format, the OSS window is one of the better times of year to be seated on the Chico Network — you get the softer games without ever registering for a tournament.

Players in Canada should note that the Chico rooms remain accessible across most provinces, and the same series schedule and bonus offers apply. If you're comparing your options north of the border, our Canada poker guide covers what to expect, while US players can check state-by-state access details on our USA hub.

The bottom line

The Online Super Series is the Chico Network's answer to the Venom, and it's a natural prompt to reassess where your bankroll should live for the season. For US and Canada players, the winning move isn't just to register for a few events — it's to pair the series run with a welcome bonus and rakeback so every hand and every buy-in during the festival is working in three directions at once.

Before the next OSS kicks off, compare the current welcome offers across all three Chico rooms on our best poker bonuses page and decide where your deposit goes to work hardest. The Main Event may be the headline, but the real edge is in how you fund the account before the first card is dealt.
