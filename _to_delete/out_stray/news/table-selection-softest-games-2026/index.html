How to Find the Softest Poker Games Online in 2026

Ask any long-term winning player where most of their profit actually comes from, and very few will point to a fancy river bluff or a hero call. The honest answer is table selection. The players you sit down against matter far more than the individual hands you play, and yet it's the one skill newcomers almost universally skip. This guide walks through exactly how to find and hold a seat in the softest games available to US and Canada players on the offshore networks. None of it requires a poker tracker or a mathematics degree — just a bit of discipline and a willingness to be picky about where you put your chips.

Why table selection beats studying strategy

Imagine two players of identical skill. One sits down at the first available table every session; the other spends thirty seconds picking the game with the loosest, most passive lineup. Over a year, the second player will crush the first — same cards, same decisions, wildly different results. That's because your win rate is not a fixed personal statistic. It's a function of the gap between you and the other people at your table. Widen that gap and everything improves: your variance shrinks, your bluffs get more respect, and your value bets get paid off.

The good news for recreational and semi-serious players is that the offshore rooms serving the US and Canada are full of casual money. These sites attract a huge pool of hobbyists who play for fun, and if you learn to seek those players out, you don't need to be a world-beater to show a profit. You just need to be consistently better than the table you chose.

The signs of a soft table

Before you take a seat, spend a moment reading the table. Most poker clients show you a few key numbers right in the lobby — average pot size and the percentage of players seeing the flop. A high average pot combined with a high flop-seeing percentage is the classic signature of a loose, action-heavy game where money changes hands freely. Those are exactly the tables you want.

  • High players-per-flop percentage — lots of people limping in and calling means lots of loose, exploitable opponents.
  • A large average pot relative to the blinds — indicates players are gambling rather than folding.
  • Short or deep-stacked recreational players — someone sitting with a random, non-standard stack size (not a clean 100 big blinds) is often a casual player rather than a regular.
  • Empty seats filling and emptying quickly — high turnover usually means recreational traffic rather than a table full of grinders.

Conversely, a table where everyone has a tidy full stack, the flop-seeing percentage is low, and the pots are small is almost certainly full of regulars nitpicking each other for scraps. Walk away from those. There is no prize for beating a table of professionals when a softer game is one click over.

Where the soft games live: WPN vs Chico

The two networks that dominate offshore traffic behave a little differently, and knowing their rhythms helps you find the good games faster. On the Winning Poker Network — home to Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker — the biggest recreational surges cluster around the network's marquee tournaments and its jackpot-style Sit & Go formats. When a large guaranteed event is running, the cash tables around it soften noticeably as busted tournament players fire up ring games.

The Chico Network — BetOnline, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag — shares one player pool across its skins, which means its cash tables tend to fill reliably during US and Canada evening hours. Chico's crowd skews recreational, particularly at low and mid stakes, and the network's anonymous tables level the playing field by preventing regulars from targeting weaker players over time. That anonymity is a genuine advantage if you're the one being hunted — but it also means you have to rely on in-the-moment reads rather than a database of notes.

Timing your sessions for maximum softness

When you play matters almost as much as where. Recreational players log on when they're relaxing — evenings and weekends, especially Friday and Saturday nights and Sunday afternoons around the big weekly tournaments. Weekday mornings, by contrast, are the domain of grinders putting in volume. If your schedule allows it, weighting your sessions toward peak recreational hours is one of the simplest edges you can give yourself.

The etiquette of leaving a bad table

Table selection isn't a one-time decision at the start of a session — it's continuous. Good players change constantly. If the two recreational players at your table bust and get replaced by tight regulars, the game you chose has stopped being profitable, and there's no shame in leaving. Chasing your losses at a table that's turned tough is how winning sessions become losing ones. Set an internal rule: when the fish leave, you leave.

Multi-tabling adds a wrinkle. It's tempting to keep every table open for the volume, but four mediocre tables will usually earn less than two great ones while costing you far more mental energy. Quality over quantity almost always wins for anyone who isn't a seasoned grinder.

Putting it all together

Table selection is the closest thing online poker has to a free lunch. It costs nothing, requires no special software, and delivers a bigger edge than most players will ever squeeze out of studying charts. Frequently asked questions

What's the single fastest way to spot a soft poker table?

Check the lobby stats for a high players-per-flop percentage and a large average pot relative to the blinds. Both together signal a loose, action-heavy game full of casual players.

Does table selection matter as much in anonymous games?

Yes. Even on anonymous tables like Chico's, you can still read live signals such as pot sizes, flop-seeing percentages, and odd stack sizes to judge whether a game is soft before you sit down.
