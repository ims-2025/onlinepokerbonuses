Bitcoin, Litecoin or Stablecoins? Best Crypto for Poker

Not all crypto is equal when funding a poker account. Here's how Bitcoin, Litecoin and stablecoins compare on fees, speed and bonus eligibility for US and

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 6, 2026·8 min read

For US and Canada players, cryptocurrency has quietly become the default way to move money in and out of offshore poker rooms. It sidesteps the card declines and bank friction that plague fiat deposits, it clears faster than almost any traditional method, and it often unlocks the biggest, dedicated welcome offers on the market. But 'crypto' isn't one thing — the coin you choose materially changes your fees, your transfer speed, and sometimes even the size of the bonus you qualify for. If you're comparing the current offers on our best poker bonuses page, it's worth understanding which coin actually serves you best before you fund an account.

The three options that dominate poker cashiers in 2026 are Bitcoin, Litecoin and stablecoins like USDT (Tether) and USDC. Each has a distinct profile. Bitcoin is the most widely accepted and the most liquid; Litecoin is the quiet workhorse favored by grinders for its speed and low fees; and stablecoins solve the one problem the other two can't — price volatility. Let's break down where each one wins.

Bitcoin: the universal default

Bitcoin is accepted at every offshore room that touches crypto, which makes it the safest choice if you simply want something that always works. All five of the rooms we cover — Americas Cardroom, BetOnline, Black Chip Poker, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag — treat Bitcoin as a first-class deposit and withdrawal method, and their largest dedicated crypto welcome bonuses are almost always available when you fund with it.

The trade-offs are network fees and confirmation times. When the Bitcoin network is busy, on-chain transaction fees can climb and confirmations can slow to tens of minutes, which is a minor annoyance when you're trying to sit down at a table. For a one-time, larger deposit it's rarely a dealbreaker. For a player who moves money frequently, those costs add up over a year. Bitcoin is the coin you reach for when reliability and acceptance matter more than shaving a few dollars off each transfer.

Litecoin: the grinder's workhorse

Ask experienced offshore players what they actually deposit with and a surprising number will say Litecoin. It behaves like Bitcoin's faster, cheaper cousin: transactions typically confirm in a couple of minutes rather than tens of minutes, and network fees are usually a tiny fraction of a busy Bitcoin transfer. For a grinder who reloads regularly, tops up mid-series, or cashes out weekly, those savings and that speed compound into a genuinely better experience.

Litecoin is now supported at all the major poker cashiers, so you're not sacrificing acceptance to get the speed. The main thing to check is bonus eligibility — some rooms tie their headline crypto offer specifically to Bitcoin or to 'crypto' broadly, and the fine print occasionally differs by coin. Before you deposit, confirm the offer applies to Litecoin, or simply follow our walkthrough on how to claim so you enter the right code for the coin you're using and don't leave bonus money on the table.

Stablecoins: killing the volatility problem

The one real downside of Bitcoin and Litecoin is price movement. If you deposit $500 worth of Bitcoin and it dips 8% while it sits in your poker bankroll, your effective balance shrinks even though you haven't lost a single hand. Stablecoins — USDT and USDC being the most common — are pegged to the US dollar, so $500 in stablecoin stays worth about $500 regardless of what the broader crypto market does that week.

That stability makes stablecoins increasingly popular for the money you intend to actually play with, as opposed to money you're speculating on. The considerations are which blockchain network you send them over — the same stablecoin can travel on different networks with very different fees and speeds — and whether your chosen room supports that specific token and network. When they line up, a stablecoin gives you crypto's speed and banking convenience without asking you to also become a currency trader. How your coin choice interacts with bonuses

This is the part casual players miss. The coin you deposit with can affect the size of the bonus you're offered, because many offshore rooms reserve their largest welcome match for crypto deposits specifically. That means funding with Bitcoin, Litecoin or a supported stablecoin doesn't just save you card-decline headaches — it can be the difference between a standard match and the biggest offer on the menu.

A few practical rules keep you on the right side of the fine print:

  • Confirm the offer covers your coin. Most crypto bonuses apply broadly, but always verify Litecoin or your chosen stablecoin qualifies before depositing.
  • Enter the correct bonus code. The single most common costly mistake is funding the account without applying the code, which can forfeit the offer entirely.
  • Mind the minimum deposit. Crypto welcome offers usually carry a minimum to qualify — deposit under it and the match won't trigger.
  • Match the coin to your habits. Occasional large deposits favor Bitcoin's simplicity; frequent reloads favor Litecoin's speed and low fees; play-money stability favors stablecoins.

Run those together and the 'best' coin becomes a personal question rather than a universal one. A recreational player making one deposit a month has different priorities than a multi-tabling grinder cashing out every week.

A quick word on cashing out

Deposits get all the attention, but withdrawals are where crypto really earns its reputation. Offshore rooms process crypto cashouts dramatically faster than checks or wire transfers, often within a day rather than the week-plus that traditional methods can take. Whatever coin you deposit with, you'll generally want to withdraw in the same one, and the same speed-and-fee logic applies: Litecoin and stablecoins tend to move faster and cheaper than Bitcoin when the network is congested. For a fuller look at the withdrawal side, our regional guides for USA and Canada players cover which rooms have the most reliable payout track records.

The bottom line

There's no single best cryptocurrency for poker — there's a best coin for your situation. Bitcoin is the safe, universally accepted default. Litecoin is the low-fee, high-speed favorite of regular grinders. Stablecoins remove the volatility risk from the money you actually want to play with. Pick the one whose strengths match how often you move money and how much volatility you're comfortable holding, and you'll get more value out of every deposit and every cashout.

Frequently asked questions

Which cryptocurrency has the lowest fees for poker deposits?

Litecoin and many stablecoin networks typically carry far lower fees than Bitcoin, especially when the Bitcoin network is busy. For frequent deposits and withdrawals, that difference adds up meaningfully over time.

Do crypto deposits qualify for bigger poker bonuses?

Often, yes. Many offshore rooms reserve their largest welcome match for crypto deposits. Always confirm the specific offer covers your chosen coin and enter the correct bonus code when you fund the account.
