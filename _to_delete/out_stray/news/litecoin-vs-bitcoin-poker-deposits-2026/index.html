Litecoin vs Bitcoin for Poker Deposits in 2026

Bitcoin is the default crypto for poker deposits, but Litecoin is often faster and cheaper. Here is how the two compare for real bankroll movement.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published September 2, 2026·8 min read

Ask most American poker players which cryptocurrency they use to move money in and out of an offshore room and the answer is Bitcoin, almost reflexively. It was the first option the rooms supported, it is the one every exchange sells, and it is the coin the cashier page tends to list at the top. But Bitcoin being the default does not make it the best tool for the specific job of funding a poker account, and a growing number of grinders have quietly switched to Litecoin for exactly that reason.

The choice matters more than it looks. If you are moving money a few times a month — a deposit here, a cashout there — the difference between the two coins compounds into real money and real waiting time over a year. This is a comparison worth making deliberately rather than defaulting into.

The three things that actually matter

Forget price charts and market capitalisation. For the narrow purpose of getting money from your wallet into a poker account and back out again, only three variables matter.

  • Transaction fee. What the network charges to process your transfer, paid on top of the amount you send.
  • Confirmation time. How long until the room credits your balance, or until a withdrawal lands in your wallet.
  • Volatility exposure. How much the value can move during the window when you are holding the coin rather than a dollar-denominated balance.

Litecoin is better on the first two. On the third, both carry the same fundamental problem, though the exposure window differs. That is the whole comparison, and the rest of this article is the detail behind it.

Fees: the difference that compounds

Bitcoin network fees are market-driven and vary enormously. When the network is quiet, sending a transaction is inexpensive. When it is congested — which happens unpredictably and sometimes for days at a time — fees can spike to levels that make small transfers genuinely uneconomic. A player moving $100 into a poker account during a congestion event can find a meaningful percentage of it consumed before it arrives.

Litecoin fees have historically been a small fraction of Bitcoin's, and crucially they are far more stable. The network was designed with faster, cheaper transfers as an explicit goal, and it has never seen the sustained fee pressure that periodically hits Bitcoin. For a player making regular modest-sized transfers, this is the single strongest argument for switching.

The practical effect is easiest to see in behaviour rather than numbers. Players using Bitcoin tend to move money in larger, less frequent chunks specifically to amortise the fee — which means keeping more money on the site than they otherwise would. Players using Litecoin can move smaller amounts more often without the fee mattering, which is better bankroll hygiene. Confirmation time: waiting for the table

Bitcoin blocks are produced roughly every ten minutes on average, and poker rooms typically require one to three confirmations before crediting a deposit. In the good case that is a ten-minute wait. In the ordinary case it is twenty to forty minutes. During congestion, when your transaction is competing for block space against higher-fee transactions, it can be considerably longer — and a low-fee transaction can sit unconfirmed for hours.

Litecoin blocks come roughly four times as often. The same confirmation requirement therefore clears in a fraction of the time, and because the network rarely congests, the variance around that estimate is much narrower. In practice a Litecoin deposit is usually credited in a few minutes.

Whether this matters depends on how you play. If you plan your sessions and deposit the day before, a thirty-minute wait is irrelevant. If you want to register for a tournament that starts in fifteen minutes, or top up mid-session after a bad run, it is the difference between playing and not playing. Late registration windows in particular do not wait for block confirmations.

Volatility: the shared weakness

Both coins carry the same structural risk, and it is one players consistently underrate. Between the moment you buy crypto and the moment the poker room converts it to a dollar balance, you are holding a volatile asset. If the price falls during that window, you deposit less than you intended. On the way out it is worse, because a withdrawal that sits in your wallet while you decide what to do with it is fully exposed until you sell.

The faster confirmation time gives Litecoin a marginally smaller exposure window on the transfer itself, but this is a second-order effect. The real risk is the holding period either side of the transfer, and that is a matter of your own habits rather than the coin's technical properties. The disciplined approach is the same for both: buy, send, and convert immediately, treating the coin purely as a transport mechanism rather than an investment.

Players who want to avoid the exposure entirely tend to move to dollar-pegged stablecoins, which most rooms now support. That eliminates volatility at the cost of introducing a different set of considerations around issuer and network choice — a trade worth weighing against the banking options listed on our USA poker guide.

Where Bitcoin still wins

This is not a one-sided comparison, and there are legitimate reasons to stay with Bitcoin.

  1. Universal support. Every poker room that takes crypto takes Bitcoin. Litecoin support is very widespread but not quite universal, and it is worth checking your specific cashier before committing to a workflow.
  2. Larger transfers dilute the fee. If you move money in big infrequent chunks, the fee is a trivial percentage and the advantage largely evaporates.
  3. Bonus eligibility is sometimes coin-specific. Some crypto deposit boosts are advertised for Bitcoin specifically. Read the terms before assuming an alternative coin qualifies.
  4. Liquidity and familiarity. Bitcoin is easier to buy, sell and move between exchanges, and mistakes are less likely with a coin you already understand.

That third point catches people out regularly. A room running a crypto deposit boost may scope it to a named coin, and depositing in another one voids the offer with no way to retroactively fix it. Check first — the process is covered step by step in our how to claim guide.

A practical recommendation

For most players moving money regularly in moderate amounts, Litecoin is the better tool. It is cheaper, faster, more predictable, and supported nearly everywhere Bitcoin is. The switch costs nothing beyond buying a different coin on the same exchange you already use.

For players who move money rarely and in size, or whose room runs Bitcoin-specific promotions worth more than the fee difference, staying with Bitcoin is entirely rational. And for anyone who wants the transport benefits without the price exposure, a stablecoin is the cleanest answer of the three.

Whichever you choose, the discipline is the same: never hold longer than you need to, always send a small test transaction to a new address before moving a real amount, and confirm the exact network the room expects. Frequently asked questions

Is Litecoin faster than Bitcoin for poker deposits?

Generally yes. Litecoin blocks are produced far more frequently, so the confirmations a poker room requires clear in minutes rather than tens of minutes.

Do all poker rooms accept Litecoin?

Most rooms that accept crypto support Litecoin, but coverage is not quite universal. Check the cashier before planning your banking around it.
