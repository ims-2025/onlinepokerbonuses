Louisiana Online Poker in 2026: Laws and Options

Louisiana has casinos, card rooms and legal sports betting, but no regulated online poker. Here is where the law stands and what players do instead.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published September 21, 2026·8 min read

Louisiana is one of the more gambling-friendly states in the American South. It has riverboat casinos, a land-based casino in New Orleans, tribal properties, video poker in bars and truck stops, a state lottery, and legal sports betting that arrived parish by parish after a local-option vote. Poker has a long, unbroken presence in the state's brick-and-mortar rooms. What Louisiana does not have — and has never had — is a licensed, state-regulated online poker market.

That gap is the whole story for anyone in Baton Rouge or Shreveport looking to play online. There is no Louisiana-licensed poker site, no state regulator issuing online poker permits, and no live bill close to changing that. Understanding why, and what the practical landscape looks like instead, is worth a few minutes before you deposit anywhere.

What Louisiana law actually says

Louisiana gambling law is built around a simple structure: specific forms of gambling are authorised by specific statutes, and everything else is prohibited. The state's gambling-by-computer provision, added in the 1990s, explicitly addresses internet gambling and makes conducting it an offence. The language is aimed squarely at operators rather than players.

Three features of the framework matter for poker players:

  • Authorisation is granular. Riverboat gaming, the New Orleans land-based casino, video draw poker, charitable gaming and sports wagering each required their own enabling legislation. Online poker has never received one.
  • Local option is baked in. Sports betting was legalised only in the parishes that voted for it, which is why the map is uneven. Any future online poker framework would likely face the same parish-by-parish approval process.
  • Enforcement targets operators. As in nearly every US state, prosecutions arising from internet gambling statutes have focused on those running the games rather than individuals playing them from home.

None of that is legal advice, and the statutes are genuinely more complex than a summary can capture. But the practical position is the same as in most of the country: there is no state-regulated online poker to join, and no realistic prospect of a player being targeted for playing.

Why regulated online poker has not arrived

The obstacles in Louisiana are the same ones that have stalled online poker in every state except the handful that already regulate it, plus one that is specific to the region.

The first is market size. Online poker is a liquidity business. A ring-fenced state pool needs enough simultaneous players to fill tables and guarantee tournaments, and Louisiana's population sits in the range where a stand-alone pool would struggle badly at anything beyond peak evening hours. The states that have made ring-fenced poker work have either larger populations or an interstate compact propping up the player pool. It is the same structural problem facing every small ring-fenced market covered in our US poker guide — without a compact, small pools produce thin games.

The second is competing priorities. The legislative energy that might have gone toward online poker went into sports betting and, later, into questions about online casino gaming. Poker consistently ranks last in that queue because it generates a fraction of the tax revenue per player that slots and sportsbooks do.

The third is the existing land-based industry. Louisiana's riverboat and casino operators are politically established and historically cautious about online products that might cannibalise floor traffic. Any bill has to satisfy them before it satisfies anyone else.

What Louisiana players actually do

In the absence of a regulated option, players in the state use offshore-licensed poker rooms — the same rooms serving most of the United States. These operators hold licences in jurisdictions outside the US, accept Louisiana players, and run the largest US-facing player pools by a wide margin.

There are five rooms that dominate the US-facing market, split across two networks:

  • Winning Poker Network (WPN) — home to Americas Cardroom and Black Chip Poker. The larger of the two networks, with the biggest tournament guarantees, including the Venom.
  • Chico Poker Network — home to BetOnline Poker, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag. Smaller pools but historically softer games and integrated sportsbook and casino wallets.

Both networks are covered in detail in our US poker guide, and the head-to-head differences in traffic, software and rake structure are the single biggest factor in which one suits a given player. A tournament grinder and a low-stakes cash player will often be better served by different networks entirely.

Banking from Louisiana

Banking is where offshore play gets practical rather than theoretical. Card deposits to offshore gambling sites are frequently declined by US-issued cards, and the decline rate has climbed steadily. Cryptocurrency has become the default method for a straightforward reason: it works reliably in both directions.

The usual pattern looks like this:

  1. Deposits. Bitcoin remains the most widely accepted, but Litecoin and Bitcoin Cash are common alternatives with lower network fees and faster confirmation. Several rooms now support stablecoins as well.
  2. Withdrawals. Crypto cashouts typically clear in hours to a couple of days once the account is verified. Cheque and wire withdrawals exist at some rooms but are slower and carry fees.
  3. Verification. Every room will require identity verification before a first withdrawal. Completing it early, rather than after you have a balance waiting, saves considerable frustration.

Bonus terms interact with banking more than players expect. Many crypto deposit boosts are only available on crypto deposits, and some rooms apply different bonus percentages depending on the method used. It is worth checking the terms before choosing how to fund an account.

Taxes and record-keeping

Gambling winnings are taxable income at the federal level regardless of where the game was hosted or whether the operator is US-licensed. Louisiana also levies state income tax, and gambling winnings are generally included in state taxable income.

Offshore rooms do not issue US tax forms, which shifts the entire record-keeping burden onto the player. Keeping a simple session log — date, room, buy-in, cash-out — costs almost nothing during the year and makes filing straightforward. Crypto adds a second layer, because converting between currencies can itself be a reportable event. A tax professional familiar with gambling income is the right answer for anyone playing meaningful volume.

What would change the picture

Realistically, Louisiana is not going to lead on online poker. The likelier path is that online casino legislation arrives first in several states, poker gets bundled in as a minor component, and interstate compacts expand enough that a mid-sized state can join a pool with real liquidity rather than starting one from scratch.

If that happens, the sequence for Louisiana would probably be: an online casino bill including poker, a local-option parish vote structure mirroring sports betting, a licensing round favouring existing riverboat operators, and then a compact application. That is a multi-year process at minimum, and nothing currently in the legislature suggests it has started.

The bottom line

Louisiana players have no regulated online poker option and are unlikely to get one soon. The offshore rooms are where the games are, crypto is where the banking works, and the practical decisions are about which network fits your game and which welcome offer actually releases value at the volume you play.

Frequently asked questions

Is online poker legal in Louisiana?

There is no state-regulated online poker in Louisiana, and the state's gambling statutes prohibit operating internet gambling. Enforcement has historically targeted operators rather than individual players.

Can Louisiana players use offshore poker sites?

Offshore-licensed rooms accept Louisiana players and represent the only realistic online option. They are not regulated by any US authority, so player protections differ from a licensed state market.
