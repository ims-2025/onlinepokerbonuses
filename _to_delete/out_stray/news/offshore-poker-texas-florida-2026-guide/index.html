Offshore Poker in Texas and Florida: 2026 Legal Guide

No regulated online poker in Texas or Florida in 2026, so millions turn to offshore rooms. Here's how the law stands and what players should know.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published August 14, 2026·8 min read

Texas and Florida are two of the largest states in the country, yet neither offers a regulated online poker room — a gap that pushes millions of would-be players toward offshore sites. If you live in either state and want to understand where things actually stand, this guide lays out the legal landscape in plain terms and explains why the best poker bonuses available to you come from offshore operators rather than in-state licensees. Note up front: this is general information, not legal advice.

The situation in both states is best understood as a distinction between what's regulated, what's prohibited, and the large gray area in between where offshore online poker sits. Let's take each state in turn.

Texas: big appetite, no regulated online poker

Texas has a famously complicated relationship with gambling. The state constitution is restrictive, live poker exists in a patchwork of membership-based card clubs operating under a specific legal theory, and casino gambling has repeatedly stalled in the legislature. Efforts to authorize expanded gaming surface every session and just as reliably fail to cross the finish line, leaving Texas without any state-licensed online poker platform.

For online play specifically, there is no Texas-regulated site to join. Players who want to play online poker from within the state therefore look to offshore rooms — operators licensed in other jurisdictions that accept Texas players. Texas law focuses its enforcement on operators rather than individual recreational players, and there is no established history of the state pursuing ordinary players for logging on to an offshore game. That's the practical reality behind why offshore sites dominate the Texas market.

Florida: tribal gaming, but not online poker

Florida's picture is shaped by a landmark tribal gaming compact that reorganized much of the state's legal betting, most visibly by bringing regulated online sports betting to Florida under a tribal umbrella. But that framework did not extend to regulated online poker or online casino games. So while Florida has moved further than Texas on regulated online gambling in one narrow category, everyday online poker remains outside the state-sanctioned system.

The result is similar to Texas in the end: a Floridian who wants to play cash games or tournaments online turns to offshore poker rooms, because there is no in-state licensed poker site to sign up with. Live poker is widely available in Florida's card rooms and tribal casinos, but that's a separate world from playing on your phone at home.

Why offshore rooms fill the gap

When a state neither licenses nor effectively blocks online poker, offshore operators step into the vacuum. The rooms most US and Canada players use run on two networks — the Winning Poker Network and the Chico Network — and accept players from states like Texas and Florida that have no regulated alternative. Sites such as Americas Cardroom on WPN and BetOnline on Chico built their player pools largely on this dynamic.

Because these rooms compete for players nationwide rather than within a single regulated state, they lean heavily on promotions to attract deposits — which is why the welcome bonuses and rakeback deals available to Texas and Florida players are often more aggressive than what a ring-fenced state market offers. What players should keep in mind

Playing on an offshore site is different from playing on a state-regulated platform, and it's worth going in clear-eyed:

  • No state consumer protections. Offshore rooms answer to their licensing jurisdiction, not a Texas or Florida regulator, so the player safeguards of a domestic market don't apply.
  • Banking runs on crypto and alternatives. Deposits and withdrawals frequently use Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, which are fast and reliable but require a little setup for newcomers.
  • Reputation is your best filter. Stick to long-established rooms with a track record of paying players, rather than unknown sites chasing quick sign-ups.
  • The law can change. Both states revisit gaming legislation regularly. What's true in 2026 may shift, so keep an eye on your state's legislative sessions.

Bonuses as the offshore advantage

The one genuine upside of the offshore model for Texas and Florida players is the promotional value. Without ring-fenced state competition to answer to, the major rooms use welcome matches, reload offers, and rakeback to win deposits. A player who understands how those offers work — how they clear against rake, when they expire, and how rakeback compounds on top — can extract meaningful value that simply isn't on the table in more tightly regulated markets. That's the trade-off in a sentence: offshore play gives up state-level oversight in exchange for a more competitive, more generous promotional environment. Whether that trade suits you is a personal decision, and it's one every Texas and Florida player effectively makes by default given the absence of a regulated option.

Funding an account from Texas or Florida

Because offshore rooms operate outside the domestic banking system, they lean on payment methods that route around it — and for most players that means cryptocurrency. Bitcoin remains the default at the major rooms, with several also supporting alternatives like Litecoin, Ethereum, and stablecoins that settle faster and carry lower network fees. The setup is a one-time hurdle: buy crypto on a mainstream exchange, send it to the poker room's cashier address, and your balance appears within minutes. Withdrawals typically return the same way, and the leading rooms have a long record of paying crypto cashouts reliably.

For newcomers in either state, the practical advice is to start small on your first deposit-and-withdrawal cycle. Move a modest amount through the full loop — deposit, play a little, cash out — before committing a larger bankroll. That confirms the process works end to end on your setup and builds confidence before you fund an account to clear a full welcome bonus. Card and voucher options exist at some rooms too, but crypto is almost always the smoothest path for Texas and Florida players.

The bottom line

As of 2026, neither Texas nor Florida licenses online poker. Florida has regulated online sports betting through its tribal compact, and Texas continues to debate expanded gaming without passing it, but in both states everyday online poker lives in the offshore market. Enforcement targets operators, not recreational players, which is why sites on the WPN and Chico networks remain the practical home for players in both states.
