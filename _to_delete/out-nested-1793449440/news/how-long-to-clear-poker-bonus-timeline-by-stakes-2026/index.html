How Long Does It Take to Clear a Poker Bonus? Timeline

Wondering how many hands or tournaments it takes to clear a poker bonus? Use this simple formula to estimate your timeline by stakes, format and schedule.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 23, 2026·8 min read

The most common question new players ask after claiming a deposit bonus is simple: how long is this going to take? A headline like 100% up to $2,000 sounds enormous, but the money does not land in your account all at once. It is released in increments as you generate rake, and the pace at which you do that depends on your stakes, your game format, how many tables you play and how much time you have each week. A realistic timeline is the difference between claiming a bonus you will actually finish and one that expires with half the value unused.

This guide gives you a plain formula for estimating your own timeline, walks through examples at different stakes, and explains the practical levers that speed things up. If you are still comparing offers, start with our best poker bonuses page, and if you want the mechanics of how release schedules work, read our primer on poker bonus types.

The basic formula

Almost every clearing schedule can be reduced to the same three numbers: the total bonus you can earn, the amount of rake or points required to release each dollar, and the rake you personally generate per hour. Divide the total rake needed by your hourly rake and you have the hours required. Then divide that by the hours you can play per week to get a calendar estimate.

Take the Winning Poker Network structure as an example. Bonus dollars on rooms like Americas Cardroom release at roughly one dollar for every 27.5 award points earned, and a maximum bonus of $2,000 therefore needs about 55,000 points. How many points you earn per hour depends on the rake you pay, so the real task is estimating your hourly rake. The Chico Network rooms use their own release schedules, covered in the review for BetOnline Poker, so always check the terms of the room you pick.

Estimating your hourly rake

Hourly rake depends on stakes, number of tables, hands per hour and the rake structure of the game. A useful way to think about it is that the same player will generate far more rake at higher stakes and across more tables, but the bonus is also released in absolute dollars, so a micro stakes grinder simply takes longer to reach a large bonus. The numbers below are illustrative assumptions, not guarantees, and you should replace them with figures from your own tracking software or the cashier history.

  • Micro stakes, one table: low rake per hour, so a large bonus can take many weeks of regular play.
  • Micro stakes, four tables: roughly four times the rake per hour, which shortens the timeline dramatically.
  • Small stakes, six tables: higher rake per hand and more hands, which is where a large bonus becomes realistic in a single month.
  • Tournaments: rake is the fee on each buy-in, so the volume of entries matters more than the buy-in size alone.

A quick sanity check is to open your account history after a normal session and note how many reward points or rake dollars you earned in an hour. Multiply by your realistic weekly hours and you have a baseline you can project forward. Even three or four honest sessions give you a far better estimate than any generic rule.

Example timelines at different stakes

Consider a hypothetical player who wants to earn a full $1,000 bonus released at a rate equivalent to one dollar per twenty-five dollars of rake. That requires about $25,000 in rake paid. A micro stakes player generating a few dollars of rake per hour at one table would need several thousand hours, which is not realistic. The same player multi-tabling six tables might multiply that rate but still need a very long stretch.

This illustrates a key point. Bonuses scale with the stakes you play, and the biggest offers are really designed for players who put in serious volume. If your volume is small, a smaller bonus, a bonus with a lower threshold, or a reload that matches your deposit size is a better fit. Levers that shorten your timeline

The first lever is table count. Moving from one table to four or six multiplies rake per hour, though only if you can maintain your play quality. The second is format. Fast-fold and short-handed games deal more hands per hour, while full-ring games deal fewer. The third is tournaments, especially during big series, because buy-in fees and rebuys add up quickly in a compressed schedule. The fourth is timing: lining up your deposit with a busy part of the calendar means more value per session.

Another lever is loyalty. Many rooms layer rakeback or tiered rewards on top of the welcome bonus, so the same rake earns you value twice. If you play regularly, it is worth reading our guide to rakeback and loyalty tiers so you see the full return on each hand rather than just the bonus piece.

Mistakes that slow you down

The most expensive mistake is chasing volume with a game you cannot beat. A bonus returns a portion of rake, but it does not cover a negative win rate. Playing stakes above your bankroll to speed up clearing is a quick way to lose more than the bonus is worth. A second mistake is ignoring the expiry. Many offers give you a fixed window, commonly around sixty days, and unreleased bonus dollars are forfeited after it. Plan backwards from the deadline and set weekly targets.

A third mistake is forgetting the terms around game weighting. Some games contribute less than others toward clearing, and some offers exclude certain formats entirely. Read the terms before you deposit, and double check how your preferred game is counted.

Build a weekly plan

Start by dividing your target rake by the number of weeks in the bonus window to find your weekly requirement. Then check how many hours that implies at your current table count. If the number is not realistic, reduce the bonus target by depositing a smaller amount or pick an offer with a gentler schedule. Track your progress every few days so a slow week does not turn into a missed deadline.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to clear a poker welcome bonus?

It varies by room, but many offers give you roughly sixty days from your first deposit. Check the exact terms for your chosen site, as unreleased bonus funds are typically forfeited after the window closes.

Do tournaments clear a bonus faster than cash games?

They can, especially during big series, because buy-in fees accumulate quickly with multiple entries. The best choice depends on the room's release rules and which format you play best.
