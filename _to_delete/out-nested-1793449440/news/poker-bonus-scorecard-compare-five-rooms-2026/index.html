Poker Bonus Scorecard: Compare Five Rooms Fairly

A simple scorecard to compare poker bonuses across ACR, BetOnline, Black Chip, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag using value, terms, banking and traffic.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 27, 2026·8 min read

Comparing poker bonuses sounds easy until you try it. One room advertises a 100% match, another shows a smaller percentage with better rakeback, and a third promises tournament tickets in place of cash. Headline numbers rarely tell you which one is actually worth your time. What you need is a repeatable method, and the simplest version is a scorecard that rates each room on the same handful of factors.

This article walks through a practical five-factor scorecard you can apply to any poker room, then shows how to think about the five rooms we cover. We avoid quoting specific current numbers because offers change often; for live figures, check our best poker bonuses page and the individual room reviews.

Why headline percentages mislead

A bonus is not free money. It is a conditional reward that you release by playing raked hands or entering tournaments. A 100% match with a slow release schedule and a short deadline can be worth less than a smaller match that clears quickly. The percentage and maximum amount tell you the ceiling, but not what share of it you will realistically collect. Our article on why large percentage bonuses mislead goes deeper into this trap.

The five-factor scorecard

Score each room from one to five on each of the factors below, then add them up. The exact weighting is up to you, but using the same method for every room keeps the comparison honest.

  1. Bonus value and release speed. How much can you earn, how is it released and how long do you have? Rooms that release in small increments as you play are usually easier to finish.
  2. Terms and restrictions. Look at wagering conditions, game restrictions, withdrawal limits and what voids a bonus. Clear terms score high; vague or restrictive terms score low.
  3. Ongoing rewards. Rakeback, loyalty tiers, leaderboards and reloads matter more over months than a one-time welcome offer.
  4. Banking and cashouts. Check deposit options, minimums, withdrawal speed and fees. Crypto support can be a major plus for US and Canada players.
  5. Traffic and game quality. A great bonus is little use if there are no games running at your stakes or the field is too tough.

Scoring bonus value and release speed

Start by working out how many raked hands or tournament points it takes to earn the full bonus. If a room asks for a volume that you cannot reach in the allowed time, the real value is only the portion you can release. Our bonus value per hour guide shows a simple way to estimate this. As a rough rule, a player at low stakes should be wary of any offer that needs far more volume than they normally play in a month.

Also check whether bonus money is released in even increments or in big chunks. Small, regular releases keep you motivated and reduce the risk that you run out of time with nothing to show. We explain the mechanics in our piece on how bonuses release in increments.

Scoring terms and restrictions

Terms are where good offers become bad ones. Read for deadlines, maximum cashout conditions, rules about multiple accounts or shared networks, and any restrictions on which games count. If you cannot find the terms easily, or they are written ambiguously, subtract points. The checklist in our guide to bonus terms red flags is a quick way to spot problems.

Scoring ongoing rewards

Many players focus on the welcome bonus and forget that rakeback and loyalty programs often decide their long-term profit. A room with a modest welcome bonus but strong, steady rakeback may beat a room with a flashy first deposit offer if you plan to play for months. Compare how each room calculates rewards, whether they use dealt or contributed rake, and whether tiers are easy to reach. Our article on dealt versus contributed rake explains why this detail matters.

How the five rooms differ in character

Without quoting current offers, we can describe how the rooms tend to differ. Americas Cardroom is known for large tournament schedules and a deep player pool, which helps with traffic. BetOnline Poker is attractive to players who like ongoing rakeback-style rewards and a connected sportsbook. Black Chip Poker, TigerGaming and SportsBetting.ag are tied to different networks and have their own software, bonus structures and traffic levels.

Software and network make a difference to your experience. Our comparison of WPN versus Chico outlines how the two networks differ in value, while the software comparison covers interface and features. Treat these as inputs to your traffic and game quality score, not as a verdict on their own.

Using the scorecard in practice

Create a simple spreadsheet with the five rooms as rows and the five factors as columns. Fill in scores after reading each room's current promotions and terms, and note the date, since offers change. Add a sixth column for personal fit: do they run the games you play, at the stakes and times you prefer? A room that scores lower overall but fits your schedule perfectly may be the better choice.

Revisit the scorecard every few months. Promotions evolve, traffic shifts and your own play changes. If you want to track progress once you have chosen, our bonus progress tracker article shows how to build a clean spreadsheet.

A quick worked example

Imagine you play mostly mid-week evenings at low stakes and deposit by Bitcoin. Room A offers a larger welcome match but releases it slowly, so you score it three for value, four for traffic and five for banking. Room B has a smaller match that releases quickly, so it earns four for value, but its evening traffic at your stakes is thin, so it scores two there. Adding the columns shows Room A slightly ahead, yet the gap is small enough that a better rakeback score for Room B could flip the result.

That is the real benefit of the scorecard: it shows you where a room wins and loses instead of hiding everything behind one number. If two rooms finish close, you can sensibly open accounts at both, claim each welcome offer in turn, and let your own results decide where to concentrate your play. Just remember to read each room's rules on multiple accounts and shared networks first, since some bonuses cannot be combined.

Final thoughts

No single room wins on every factor, which is exactly why a scorecard helps. It forces you to look beyond the headline and weigh the details that decide whether you actually collect a bonus. Use the same method for every room, write down your scores, and trust the totals over the marketing.

Frequently asked questions

What matters most when comparing poker bonuses?

Release speed and terms usually matter more than the headline percentage. A bonus you can realistically clear within the deadline beats a bigger one you cannot finish.

How often should I re-check my room comparison?

Every few months, or whenever a room changes its promotions. Offers, rakeback programs and traffic levels shift regularly, so an old scorecard can become misleading.
