Online Poker in Utah and Hawaii: What Players Should Know

Utah and Hawaii have some of the strictest gambling laws in the US. See how online poker is treated there and what players should weigh before depositing.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 24, 2026·7 min read

Most conversations about US online poker focus on the handful of states that license it, or on the large group of states where offshore rooms accept players in a legal gray area. Utah and Hawaii belong to neither camp. They are the two states most associated with having essentially no legal commercial gambling at all, and that shapes everything about how online poker is viewed there. If you live in one of them, or you are researching the full map of the US market, it helps to understand where the law stands and why these states stand apart.

This article is general information, not legal advice. Laws change, enforcement varies, and anyone with specific questions should speak to a qualified attorney in their state. What we can do is lay out the broad picture so you can make informed decisions and know what to look for. For the national context, start with our USA player guide.

Why Utah and Hawaii are different

Almost every US state allows at least some form of gambling, whether a state lottery, tribal casinos, racetracks, or charitable games. Utah and Hawaii are widely described as the exceptions: both lack a state lottery and both lack commercial casinos. Their legal frameworks are built on broad prohibitions rather than carve-outs, and their statutes tend to define gambling in wide terms.

That matters for poker because many states that tolerate home games or social play do so through specific exemptions. Where the baseline rule is a general ban, there is less room for ambiguity about whether playing for money online is treated as unlawful in principle. The practical question for players is how those laws are applied to individuals, which is a separate issue from what the statutes say.

How the statutes are generally framed

In both states, laws focus on prohibiting gambling activity, promoting it, or operating gambling businesses. Language varies, and the details of how courts and prosecutors interpret online play are not always settled. Some statutes target those who run or profit from gambling operations, while others also address players. Because of this, it is dangerous to assume that a lack of publicized prosecutions means an activity is permitted.

  • Utah is known for a constitutional and statutory approach that broadly prohibits gambling, with limited exceptions.
  • Hawaii similarly restricts most gambling, and efforts to legalize casinos or lotteries have repeatedly failed to pass.
  • Neither state has a licensed, regulated online poker market that residents can use.

Because there is no in-state licensed option, residents who want to play online poker are looking at unlicensed or offshore operators, which sit outside the protections state-regulated sites provide. We explain how that offshore model works in our guide to regulated US poker states versus offshore sites.

The federal layer

Federal law adds another dimension. Statutes such as the Wire Act and the Unlawful Internet Gambling Enforcement Act are often cited in discussions of online poker. UIGEA, in particular, is aimed at gambling businesses and the financial institutions that process payments rather than at individual players, and it does not itself make playing a crime. The Wire Act's scope has been debated for years. We cover both in our plain-English explainer on the Wire Act and UIGEA.

The key takeaway is that federal law does not replace state law. A player can be on the right side of one and the wrong side of the other. In states with broad prohibitions like Utah and Hawaii, state rules are the more important piece of the puzzle for residents.

What this means for offshore poker

Offshore poker rooms such as those we cover generally state in their terms which jurisdictions they accept. Some rooms restrict players from certain states, and their lists can change. Always read the current terms and the restricted-region section before you create an account, and do not assume that being able to register means an operator accepts a given location. A room can also close accounts and withhold funds if it determines a player was in a prohibited location.

Our reviews note location coverage where it is known, but policies change, so verify directly with the operator. You can read about eligibility and terms in the Americas Cardroom review and the BetOnline Poker review, and see how state-by-state access is generally handled on our USA page.

Risks to weigh honestly

Even setting aside the legal question, there are practical risks with any unlicensed operator. There is no state regulator to appeal to if a dispute arises, no mandated segregation of player funds, and limited recourse if a room stops paying out. These are general risks of the offshore model, not claims about any specific brand, and they apply no matter where you live. In a state that prohibits gambling outright, you also have to consider that the legal risk sits with you personally.

  • Check whether your location is listed as restricted in the operator's terms.
  • Never use a VPN to disguise your location. Most rooms prohibit it and may confiscate balances. See our piece on VPNs and geolocation risks.
  • Only deposit money you can afford to lose and consider setting deposit limits.
  • Keep records of deposits and withdrawals for tax purposes.

Responsible play matters in every jurisdiction. If gambling is causing problems for you or someone you know, resources and self-exclusion tools exist, and we cover them in our guide to responsible play and deposit limits.

Could this ever change?

Legislators in both states have periodically discussed gambling proposals, and regulated online poker has been debated in many other states. But Utah and Hawaii have a long history of rejecting expansion, and there is no clear signal that a licensed online poker market is coming soon. Anyone hoping for a change should follow official legislative sources rather than rumors.

In the meantime, the most useful thing a player can do is stay informed, understand the legal and practical risks, and make decisions with clear eyes. Frequently asked questions

Are Utah and Hawaii the only states with no legal gambling?

They are the two most commonly cited as having no state lottery and no commercial casinos. Laws change, so check current sources or consult an attorney for the latest status.

Does federal law make it illegal for individuals to play online poker?

UIGEA targets gambling businesses and payment processors rather than individual players, but federal law does not override state law, so your state's rules still matter. This is general information, not legal advice.
