Which Poker Games Count Toward Clearing a Bonus in 2026?

Not every hand earns bonus progress. See how cash games, tournaments, fast-fold and heads-up play are treated when clearing a poker bonus in 2026.

OnlinePokerBonuses Editorial Team·Published October 30, 2026·8 min read

Most poker bonus disappointments do not come from the headline percentage. They come from a quiet clause in the terms: the game you spent two weeks grinding did not count, or counted at a fraction of the usual rate. A player who deposits, claims a welcome offer and then plays the format they enjoy most can end up with a bonus that barely moves. Knowing which games contribute, and how much, is one of the easiest ways to protect the value of an offer before you spend a single chip.

This guide explains the general rules of game eligibility at online poker rooms serving US and Canadian players. Every room writes its own terms, so treat the patterns below as a framework for reading the fine print, not a replacement for it. If you are still choosing where to play, start with our best poker bonuses comparison and then check the specific promotion page before you deposit.

How bonus clearing actually works

Poker bonuses are almost never paid as a lump sum. They are typically released in increments as you generate rake or tournament fees, which are the money the room collects from each pot or entry. You are not wagering your own chips against the house, as you might with a casino bonus. Instead, the room is rewarding you for contributing to its revenue. That is why the same bonus can be easy to clear for one player and nearly impossible for another: the speed depends on how much rake you generate, not on how much you win or lose.

Because the whole system depends on rake, the key question is always the same: does the game you are playing produce rake that the bonus counts? Some formats take rake but are excluded from bonus clearing. Others count only a fraction. A few do not take rake at all, so they cannot contribute no matter what the terms say. Our guide to bonus types explains the main structures if you want the foundation first.

Cash games: usually the safest route

Regular ring games, especially no-limit hold'em at standard stakes, are the format most bonus terms were written around. Rake from these pots is tracked precisely, and in many rooms either dealt or contributed rake can be credited toward progress. If your main goal is to clear a bonus, cash games at low and mid stakes tend to be the most predictable path.

There are still details to check. Some offers exclude the smallest stakes because rake there is so thin, while others exclude very high stakes to discourage abuse. Heads-up tables are the most common cash-game exclusion, because two players can generate rake quickly and the room wants to limit pairs of accounts passing chips back and forth. Short-handed and full-ring tables generally count equally, but always confirm.

Fast-fold and speed formats

Fast-fold games, where you are moved to a new table the moment you fold, produce many more hands per hour. That can be a real advantage for clearing a bonus, because more hands mean more chances to pay rake. Some rooms count these games at full value, some at a reduced rate, and a few exclude them. The effect on your clearing speed can be large, so it is worth reading the clause rather than assuming. For a deeper look, see our guide to clearing bonuses with fast-fold poker.

Remember that a high hand count is only helpful if you can play profitably at a faster pace. If you tilt or misread the game under time pressure, you may lose more at the tables than the bonus is worth. Match the format to your skill, not just to the clearing maths.

Tournaments, sit and gos and freerolls

Tournament fees are a different pool of money from cash-game rake. Some bonuses count the fee portion of every buy-in, some count it at a lower rate, and some ignore tournaments entirely. Players who prefer multi-table events should pay particular attention to this, because a welcome offer tuned for cash-game players can be slow going for a pure tournament grinder. The fee, not the prize pool contribution, is what usually counts. If you are not sure how your buy-ins break down, our article on tournament fees and rake explains what you are really paying.

Satellites and freerolls are the usual exceptions. Satellites often contribute at a reduced rate or not at all, and freerolls have no entry fee to count. Special series, such as large online festivals, sometimes carry their own promotional rules that override the general terms. If your plan involves a big series, check that the buy-ins you intend to play will count.

Other poker variants and casino-style games

Omaha, mixed games and other variants can sometimes be used for clearing, but the contribution rate varies. Pot-limit Omaha is a good example: the pots grow larger faster, which can mean more rake per hand, though some terms apply a reduced weight. Our article on clearing poker bonuses with PLO versus hold'em covers the trade-offs.

Casino-style games and sportsbook bets generally do not count toward a poker bonus, and in the reverse direction poker play usually does not count toward a casino bonus. Some rooms offer a combined account with separate welcome packages for each product. In that case, each bonus has its own clearing rules, and activity in one product does nothing for the other.

How to check eligibility before you deposit

A short checklist saves a lot of frustration. Before you accept a promotion, look for the following in the terms:

  • Eligible games and stakes: which formats and limits count, and whether any are excluded.
  • Contribution rates: whether some games count at a reduced percentage.
  • Rake basis: whether the room credits dealt rake, contributed rake or tournament fees.
  • Time limit: how long you have to clear the bonus before it expires.
  • Restrictions on heads-up play and chip dumping: rules that can void the offer if broken.

If the terms are vague, contact support in advance and ask for the answer in writing. A screenshot of a chat or an email reply is worth a great deal if you later need to dispute a missing credit. Choosing a room that fits how you play

The best bonus is the one your preferred format actually clears. A tournament specialist might do better with a room that credits tournament fees generously, while a cash-game grinder should look for broad stake eligibility and full credit for fast-fold. Frequently asked questions

Do tournaments count toward poker bonus clearing?

It depends on the room and the offer. Some bonuses count tournament fees fully, some at a reduced rate, and some only credit cash-game rake, so always check the eligible games section of the promotion terms.

Why are heads-up games often excluded from poker bonuses?

Heads-up tables let two accounts generate rake quickly, which makes them attractive for bonus abuse and chip dumping. Many rooms exclude them or reduce their contribution to protect the integrity of the promotion.
